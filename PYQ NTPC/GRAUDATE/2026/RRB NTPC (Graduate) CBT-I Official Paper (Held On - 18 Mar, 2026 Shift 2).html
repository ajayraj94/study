📌 Key Points

  • Fundamental Duties are enshrined in Part IV-A of the Constitution of India under Article 51A. These duties were added to the constitution to remind citizens of their social and moral obligations towards the nation.
  • Article 51A(h): This clause states that it is the duty of every citizen to develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform. This duty is specifically designed to promote scientific values and rational thinking among the masses. Hence, this statement is correct.
  • Article 51A(f): This clause emphasizes the duty to value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture. This is the primary duty associated with the protection and promotion of cultural values and historical legacy. Hence, this statement is correct.
  • Article 51A(i): This clause mandates citizens to safeguard public property and to abjure violence.   • Article 51A(j): This clause encourages citizens to strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavor and achievement. 📌 Additional Information

  • Origin and Adoption: The Fundamental Duties were not part of the original Constitution. They were incorporated based on the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee during the Internal Emergency in 1976. The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 introduced 10 duties initially.
  • Source: The concept of Fundamental Duties in the Indian Constitution was inspired by the constitution of the erstwhile USSR (now Russia). Unlike Fundamental Rights, these duties are non-justiciable, meaning they cannot be directly enforced by courts, though they serve as a guide for interpreting laws.
  • The 11th Duty: Originally there were only 10 duties. The 11th Fundamental Duty, which requires parents or guardians to provide opportunities for education to children between the age of 6 and 14 years, was added by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act in the year 2002.
  • Verma Committee (1999): This committee was established to identify the existence of legal provisions for the implementation of some of the Fundamental Duties. It pointed out that several duties are already enforceable through existing laws like the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971) and the Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955).

📌 Important Points

  • Significance of Scientific Temper: The inclusion of scientific temper as a duty is unique to the Indian Constitution. It encourages a questioning mind and discourages superstition, aiming for a modern, progressive India.
  • Preservation of Culture: The term "Composite Culture" refers to the syncretic and diverse traditions of India, including various religions, languages, and ethnicities, which citizens are expected to protect.
  • Public Property and Violence: Article 51A(i) is crucial for maintaining internal security and social harmony, especially during protests or civil unrest, ensuring that national assets are not destroyed.

📌 Key Points

  • The Performance tab in Windows Task Manager is specifically designed to provide real-time visualization of the hardware resources being utilized by the system.
  • It displays dynamic CPU (Central Processing Unit) usage graphs, showing the current utilization percentage and clock speed (measured in GHz).
  • This tab also provides detailed memory usage graphs, which track how much RAM (Random Access Memory) is currently in use, available, and cached.
  • Beyond just CPU and RAM, the Performance tab monitors Disk activity (Read/Write speeds), Ethernet/Wi-Fi throughput (Send/Receive speeds), and GPU (Graphics Processing Unit) load.
  • Users can view technical specifications such as the number of Cores, Logical processors, L1/L2/L3 cache sizes, and the system Uptime (the duration since the last reboot).

📌 Additional Information

  • Startup Tab:
    • This tab manages applications that automatically launch when the Windows operating system starts.
    • It helps users identify Startup Impact, which can be High, Medium, or Low, allowing them to disable unnecessary programs to speed up the boot process.
  • Users Tab:
    • This section lists all active user accounts currently logged into the computer.
    • It provides a breakdown of resource consumption per user, which is essential for administrators managing multi-user environments or servers.
  • Details Tab:
    • The Details tab is an advanced version of the Processes tab, providing the PID (Process ID) for every running executable.
    • It allows users to change Process Priority (e.g., High, Realtime) and set CPU Affinity to restrict a process to specific processor cores.
  • Processes Tab:
    • The primary tab that lists all Apps, Background processes, and Windows processes currently running.
    • It is the main interface used to End Task for frozen or non-responsive applications.

📌 Important Points

  • Shortcuts: The Task Manager can be quickly accessed by pressing Ctrl + Shift + Esc or by right-clicking the Taskbar in Windows 10/11.
  • Resource Monitor: For more granular analysis, the Performance tab includes a link to the Resource Monitor, which offers kernel-level details about disk I/O, network connections, and memory handles.
  • Evolution: While Task Manager has been a part of Windows since version 3.0, the modern graphical interface with the Performance tab was significantly enhanced in Windows 8 and Windows 10.
  • System Information: The Performance tab is vital for troubleshooting hardware bottlenecks and identifying if the CPU is throttling or if the system is running out of physical RAM.

In 2025, which organization released the Global Carbon Budget, providing the most comprehensive annual assessment of global carbon sources, sinks, and emission trends?

The correct answer is Global Carbon Project (GCP).

📌 Key Points

  • The Global Carbon Project (GCP) is an international research project that provides the most comprehensive annual assessment of the state of the global carbon cycle through its Global Carbon Budget report.
  • The Global Carbon Budget tracks the trends in global carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions, including those from fossil fuel combustion and land-use changes such as deforestation.
  • It quantifies the carbon sinks, which are the reservoirs that absorb carbon, including the ocean and terrestrial biosphere (land), to determine how much CO2 remains in the atmosphere each year.
  • The data provided by the GCP is critical for monitoring progress toward the goals of the Paris Agreement, which aims to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius, preferably 1.5 degrees Celsius.
  • The report is typically published in the peer-reviewed journal Earth System Science Data and involves a massive collaboration of over 100 scientists from dozens of international organizations.

📌 Additional Information

  • Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC):
    • The IPCC is a United Nations body established in 1988 to assess the science related to climate change.
    • Unlike the GCP, which releases annual data updates, the IPCC produces massive Assessment Reports every 5 to 7 years (e.g., AR6 completed in 2023).
    • It does not conduct original research but synthesizes existing peer-reviewed literature to inform policymakers globally.
  • World Meteorological Organization (WMO):
    • The WMO is a specialized agency of the UN dedicated to meteorology, operational hydrology, and geophysical sciences.
    • It releases the State of the Global Climate report, which focuses on temperature increases, glacier retreat, and sea-level rise, rather than the specific carbon budget breakdown.
    • The WMO is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, and facilitates the exchange of weather data across 193 Member States.
  • UN Environment Programme (UNEP):
    • UNEP is responsible for coordinating the United Nations' environmental activities and assisting developing countries in implementing environmentally sound policies.
    • It is well known for publishing the annual Emissions Gap Report, which analyzes the difference between where greenhouse gas emissions are predicted to be and where they should be to avoid the worst impacts of climate change.

📌 Important Points

  • Origins and Structure: The Global Carbon Project was established in 2001 as a partnership between the International Geosphere-Biosphere Programme, the World Climate Research Programme, and others.
  • Fossil Fuel Trends: Recent reports from the GCP indicate that global fossil CO2 emissions reached record highs in 2023 and 2024, driven largely by coal, oil, and natural gas consumption.
  • Geographic Contributions: The budget breaks down emissions by country, highlighting the roles of major emitters such as China, the United States, India, and the European Union.
  • Negative Emissions: The GCP also assesses the effectiveness of Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR) technologies, noting that current nature-based and technological removals are currently insufficient to offset global emissions.
  • Data Transparency: All data generated by the GCP is open access, allowing researchers and NGOs to verify carbon footprints and national inventory reports.

  • Judicial Review: In the landmark L. Chandra Kumar case (1997), the Supreme Court ruled that the decisions of these tribunals are subject to the writ jurisdiction of the High Courts under Article 226 and Article 227. Consequently, an appeal against the CAT order lies before a Division Bench of the concerned High Court.

📌 Important Points

  • Function and Purpose: The primary function of CAT is to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to the employees of the Central Government, All India Services, and notified local bodies or corporations. It aims to reduce the burden on regular civil courts.
  • Legal Representation: An applicant can appear in person before the Tribunal or may be represented by a legal practitioner. The application fee is a nominal amount of Rs. 50, ensuring that the process is affordable for all levels of public servants.
  • Historical Context: Before the establishment of CAT in November 1985, aggrieved government servants had to approach High Courts or Subordinate Courts, which resulted in prolonged delays and high costs. The Administrative Tribunals Act was enacted by Parliament specifically to fulfill the mandate of Article 323A.
