Explanation:

The correct answer is James Princep.

📌 Key Points

  • James Princep was an English scholar, orientalist, and an officer in the Calcutta Mint of the East India Company.
  • In the year 1837, he successfully deciphered the Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts, which were used in the earliest inscriptions and coins of ancient India.
  • Through his research, he identified that the inscriptions referring to a king named Devanampiya Piyadasi (meaning "Beloved of the Gods" and "of Pleasant Gaze") were actually referring to the Mauryan Emperor Ashoka.
  • This breakthrough allowed historians to correlate archaeological evidence with Buddhist chronicles like the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa, establishing Ashoka as one of the greatest rulers in Indian history. Hence, the statement regarding James Princep is correct.
  • The identification of these inscriptions provided a chronological framework for ancient Indian history, which was previously based largely on legends and myths.

📌 Additional Information

  • R C Majumdar:
    • Ramesh Chandra Majumdar (1888–1980) was a prominent Indian historian known for his monumental work "The History and Culture of the Indian People".
    • While he contributed extensively to the study of ancient India and Bengal, he was not the one who deciphered the Brahmi script.
  • Romila Thapar:
    • Romila Thapar is a world-renowned Indian historian whose primary focus is Ancient India.
    • She is well-known for her book "Ashoka and the Decline of the Mauryas", published in 1961, which offers a socio-economic analysis of the Mauryan Empire.
    • Her work interprets the Dhamma of Ashoka as a civic code rather than a religious doctrine.
  • Susan Bayly:
    • Susan Bayly is a professor of Historical Anthropology at the University of Cambridge.
    • Her research focuses on caste, society, and colonialism in South Asia and Southeast Asia, particularly Vietnam.
    • She is the author of "Caste, Society and Politics in India from the Eighteenth Century to the Modern Age".
  • Brahmi Script:
    • It is the ancestor of almost all modern Indian scripts and was used by Ashoka for his Rock Edicts and Pillar Edicts.
    • The script was primarily written from left to right, and its decipherment opened the door to understanding Magadhi Prakrit.

📌 Important Points

  • Epigraphy: The study of inscriptions is known as epigraphy. The decipherment by James Princep is considered the most significant milestone in Indian epigraphy.
  • Maski Inscription: Although James Princep linked Piyadasi to Ashoka, the final confirmation came in 1915 when an inscription was found at Maski in Karnataka that explicitly mentioned the name "Ashoka" alongside "Devanampiya".
  • Asiatic Society: James Princep served as the founding editor of the Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal, which published his findings on Ashokan pillars located in Delhi and Allahabad.
  • Kharosthi Script: Unlike Brahmi, Kharosthi was written from right to left and was used primarily in the Northwest regions of the Indian subcontinent, such as Gandhara (modern-day Pakistan and Afghanistan).

  • The central theme of the novel is the plight of widows in contemporary Hindu society.   • Year of Publication: 1857 is a landmark year in Indian history due to the Great Revolt, and the publication of this novel in the same year highlights that social revolutions were occurring alongside political upheavals.
  • Religious Influence: The novel also acted as a piece of Christian apologetics, suggesting that the liberation of women could be found through the adoption of Christian values, which was a common theme among 19th-century converts.

Question No. 7Test (Q.7)
Which of the following administrative features was a key characteristic of the Mauryan Empire under Chandragupta Maurya?
A)
Centralised bureaucracy guided by Arthashastra  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
Hereditary provincial governors
C)
Feudal land grant system
D)
Revenue collection by religious institutions
Correct Option: A) Centralised bureaucracy guided by Arthashastra
Explanation:

The correct answer is Centralised bureaucracy guided by Arthashastra.

📌 Key Points

  • The Mauryan Empire, founded by Chandragupta Maurya in approximately 322 BCE, was characterized by a highly sophisticated and centralised bureaucracy. This system ensured that the central government had direct control over various aspects of the state, from revenue to security. Hence, statement 1 is correct.
  • The administrative framework was heavily influenced by the Arthashastra, a classic treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy authored by Chanakya (also known as Kautilya or Vishnugupta), who was the Prime Minister and mentor to Chandragupta Maurya.
  • The empire was divided into provinces, usually headed by a Kumara (prince) or a member of the royal family. Unlike later medieval systems, these provincial governors were appointed officials and not hereditary rulers, ensuring the King's authority remained absolute. Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.
  • The Mauryan economy was largely state-controlled, and land revenue was the primary source of income. The feudal land grant system, where land was given to intermediaries in exchange for military service, was a feature that developed much later during the Gupta period and the early medieval era. Hence, statement 3 is incorrect.
  • Revenue collection was a strictly secular and administrative function carried out by state officials like the Samaharta (Chief Collector). Religious institutions did not have the authority to collect state taxes; instead, they were often recipients of state patronage. Hence, statement 4 is incorrect.

📌 Additional Information

  • Structure of the Central Government:
    • The King was the fountainhead of all authority, assisted by a council of ministers called the Mantriparishad.
    • The bureaucracy was composed of a hierarchy of officials known as Amatyas (civil servants) and Adhyakshas (superintendents of various departments).
    • There were nearly 27-30 departments managing diverse sectors like agriculture (Sita-adhyaksha), commerce (Panyadhyaksha), and weights and measures (Pautavadhyaksha).
  • Municipal Administration:
    • The Greek ambassador Megasthenes, in his work Indica, provides a detailed account of the administration of the capital city, Pataliputra.
    • The city was managed by six committees of five members each, looking after industries, foreigners, births and deaths, trade, manufactured goods, and the collection of the tenth of the prices as tax.
  • The Espionage System:
    • A unique feature of the Mauryan state was its extensive network of spies, referred to as Gudha Purushas.
    • Spies were categorized into Sanstha (stationary) and Sanchari (wandering), reporting directly to the King about the loyalty of officials and the pulse of the public.
  • Military Strength:
    • The Mauryan army was massive, consisting of infantry, cavalry, chariots, and elephants. Pliny and Megasthenes mention that Chandragupta Maurya maintained an army of 6,00,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry, and 9,000 elephants.
    • The military was administered by a board of 30 members divided into six committees of five members each.

📌 Important Points

  • Saptanga Theory: Kautilya described the state as having seven limbs (Saptanga): Swami (The King), Amatya (The Ministers), Janapada (The Territory/People), Durga (The Fortification), Kosha (The Treasury), Danda (The Army), and Mitra (The Ally).
  • Diplomatic Relations: Chandragupta Maurya defeated the Greek general Seleucus Nicator in 305 BCE, leading to a treaty where the Mauryan Empire gained territories like Arachosia (Kandahar) and Gedrosia (Baluchistan).
  • Public Welfare: Despite being highly centralized, the state focused on public works, including the construction of the Royal Road (precursor to the Grand Trunk Road) and irrigation projects like the Sudarshana Lake in Junagadh, Gujarat.

  • According to Article 239A of the Constitution of India, Parliament has the power to create a local legislature or a Council of Ministers (or both) for certain Union Territories like Puducherry.
  • The administrative structure of Puducherry is governed by The Government of Union Territories Act, 1963, which outlines the functions and powers of the Legislative Assembly.

📌 Additional Information

  • Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Established as a Union Territory in 1956, it is located in the Bay of Bengal and is administered by a Lieutenant Governor without a local legislature.

📌 Important Points

  • Unlike States, the Union Territories are considered federal units that are under the direct control and supervision of the Union Government of India.

  • In the case of a spring, the potential energy is given by the formula U = 1/2 kx², where k is the spring constant (a measure of the stiffness of the spring) and x is the displacement from the equilibrium position.
  • At the equilibrium position, the displacement x is zero, which means the potential energy is at its minimum (zero). Hence, the statement that potential energy increases when moving away from equilibrium is correct.
  • When a spring is compressed, work is done against the restoring force of the spring. This work is stored in the form of potential energy. Since the displacement x is squared in the formula, whether the spring is compressed (negative x) or stretched (positive x), the value of x² is always positive, leading to an increase in energy. Hence, the correct answer is that it will increase.
  • If the potential energy were to decrease, it would mean the spring is returning toward its equilibrium point from a deformed state. Hence, the statement that potential energy decreases during compression is incorrect.
  • The restoring force exerted by the spring is proportional to the displacement but in the opposite direction, described by Hooke’s Law as F = -kx.

📌 Additional Information

  • Hooke’s Law: Proposed by Robert Hooke in 1676, this law states that for relatively small deformations of an object, the displacement or size of the deformation is directly proportional to the deforming force or load.
  • Spring Constant (k): The SI unit for the spring constant is Newton per meter (N/m). A higher value of k indicates a stiffer spring that requires more force to compress or extend.
  • Conservation of Energy: In an ideal system without friction or air resistance, the mechanical energy (the sum of kinetic energy and potential energy) remains constant. When a compressed spring is released, the potential energy is converted into kinetic energy as the spring moves back toward equilibrium.
  • Applications of Spring Energy: This principle is utilized in various mechanical devices such as clocks, shock absorbers in vehicles, trampolines, and recoil mechanisms in firearms.
  • Work Done: The work done to compress a spring by a distance x is equal to the area under the Force-Displacement graph, which forms a triangle with area 1/2 × base × height, resulting in 1/2 × x × kx = 1/2 kx².

  • Conservative Force: The force exerted by a spring is a conservative force, meaning the work done by or against the force depends only on the initial and final positions and not on the path taken.
  • Elastic Limit: If a spring is compressed or stretched beyond its elastic limit, it will not return to its original shape and the Hooke's Law relation fails. This results in permanent deformation or plasticity.

  • The principle of a simple microscope is based on the fact that when an object is placed between the optical center and the principal focus of a convex lens, the image formed is virtual, erect, and magnified.
  • The magnifying power of a simple microscope is defined by the formula M = 1 + (D/f), where D is the least distance of distinct vision (standardized at 25 cm for a normal human eye) and f is the focal length of the lens.
  • These instruments are commonly used by watchmakers, jewelers, and students to observe tiny parts or organisms. Because it uses only one lens, the magnification is limited compared to a compound microscope.

    • A biconcave lens is a diverging lens that is thinner at the center than at the edges.
    • It is primarily used in the correction of Myopia (nearsightedness), as it helps spread out light rays before they reach the eye.
    • It always forms a virtual, erect, and diminished image, regardless of the object's position.
  • Cylindrical Lens:
    • A cylindrical lens focuses light into a line rather than a point.
    • It is specifically used to correct Astigmatism, a vision condition caused by an irregular curvature of the cornea or lens.
    • These lenses have different refractive powers in different planes.
  • Plano-concave Lens:
    • This lens has one flat (plano) surface and one inward-curving (concave) surface.
    • It has a negative focal length and is used to expand light beams or increase focal lengths in optical systems.

  • Reduction is a chemical reaction that involves the gaining of electrons or the removal of oxygen from a substance; it is the chemical opposite of oxidation and does not lead to the spoilage associated with rancidity. Hence, the statement regarding reduction is incorrect.

The correct answer is System software.
📌 Key Points
  • System software is a type of computer program that is designed to run a computer's hardware and application programs. It acts as the foundational layer that manages the internal resources of a computer system.
  • The Operating System (OS) is the most critical component of system software. It manages the CPU, memory, and storage, while providing a user interface (UI) and a platform for other applications to run. Examples include Microsoft Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS.
  • Device drivers are specialized system software programs that allow the operating system to communicate with specific hardware devices. Without these drivers, the OS would not be able to send or receive data from hardware like printers, scanners, graphics cards, or network adapters.
  • System software is generally pre-installed on a computer or installed during the setup of the OS. It operates in the background and is usually not directly interacted with by the end-user, unlike application software.
  • Another category of system software includes programming language translators such as compilers, interpreters, and assemblers, which convert high-level programming code into machine language that the processor can execute.
📌 Additional Information
  • Application Software: This refers to programs designed to perform specific tasks for the user. Unlike system software, these are user-oriented. Examples include word processors (like MS Word), web browsers (like Google Chrome), spreadsheets, and media players.
  • Utility Software: Often considered a sub-type of system software, utility software helps to analyze, configure, optimize, or maintain a computer. Common examples include antivirus programs, disk defragmenters, file compression tools (like WinZip), and backup utilities.
  • Firmware: This is a specific type of software that is embedded directly into a hardware device (usually on ROM or EPROM). It provides low-level control for the device's specific hardware. The BIOS (Basic Input/Output System) or UEFI on a motherboard is the most common example of firmware.
  • Temporary Programs: This is not a standard technical category of software. While some software may be ephemeral or run temporarily in RAM, the term does not define a functional category like System or Application software.
  • Entertainment Software: This is a sub-category of application software specifically designed for leisure. This includes video games and streaming applications like Netflix or Spotify.
📌 Important Points
  • Kernel: The kernel is the core part of the operating system that manages system calls and hardware abstraction. It is the first program loaded into memory when the computer starts up.
  • User Interface (UI): System software provides either a Command Line Interface (CLI), where users type text commands, or a Graphical User Interface (GUI), which uses icons and windows for easier navigation.
  • BIOS/UEFI: These are the first pieces of system software to run when a PC is powered on. They perform a Power-On Self-Test (POST) to ensure all hardware is functioning correctly before handing control over to the Operating System.
  • Resource Allocation: One of the primary functions of system software is multitasking management, which ensures that multiple application programs can share the CPU and RAM without crashing the system.
