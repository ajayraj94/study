A)
50&deg; , 130&deg;
B)
50&deg; , 60&deg;
C)
60&deg; , 50&deg;  ✓ (Correct Answer)
D)
60&deg;, 120&deg;
Correct Option: C) 60&deg; , 50&deg;
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Apply the Alternate Segment Theorem: Angle between tangent and chord = Angle in the alternate segment.

For chord PX and tangent AP: ∠PYX = ∠APX = 50°.

For chord PY and tangent BP: ∠PXY = ∠BPY = 60°.

Required values: ∠PXY = 60° and ∠PYX = 50°.

∴ Answer = 60°, 50°

Alternate Method

Given:

Tangent AB touches circle C at point P. Points X and Y are on the circle. ∠APX = 50° and ∠BPY = 60°.

Formula used:

Alternate Segment Theorem: The angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact is equal to the angle subtended by the chord in the alternate segment.

Calculations:

According to the Alternate Segment Theorem:

⇒ Angle between tangent BP and chord PY = Angle subtended by chord PY at point X

⇒ ∠BPY = ∠PXY

⇒ ∠PXY = 60°

⇒ Angle between tangent AP and chord PX = Angle subtended by chord PX at point Y

⇒ ∠APX = ∠PYX

⇒ ∠PYX = 50°

∴ The correct answer is 60°, 50°.

📌 Additional Information

Alternate Segment Theorem

For any chord of a circle, the angle it makes with a tangent through one of its endpoints is equal to the angle subtended by the chord in the alternate segment.

Tangent-Radius Property

A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact (Angle between radius and tangent = 90°).

Angle in Semicircle

The angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circumference of a circle is always 90°.

Angles in the Same Segment

Angles subtended by the same arc (or chord) at any points on the remaining part of the circle are equal.

📌 Key Points

  • Article 5 of the Indian Constitution provides for citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution, which took place on January 26, 1950.
  • According to this Article, every person who had their domicile in the territory of India at that time and fulfilled one of the three conditions became a citizen of India.
  • The specific condition mentioned in the question is found in Article 5(b), which states that a person becomes a citizen if either of whose parents was born in the territory of India. Hence, the statement regarding parentage as a basis for citizenship is correct under Article 5.
  • The other two conditions under Article 5 include being born in the territory of India (Article 5a) or being ordinarily resident in India for not less than five years immediately preceding the commencement (Article 5c).
  • This Article was essential to define the initial body of citizens for the newly formed Republic of India following the Partition of 1947.

📌 Additional Information

  • Article 6: This article deals with the rights of citizenship of certain persons who have migrated to India from Pakistan. It sets a cutoff date of July 19, 1948, for registration and residency requirements. Hence, Article 6 is not the correct option as it pertains specifically to migration from Pakistan.
  • Article 8: This provision grants citizenship rights to persons of Indian origin residing outside India (in foreign countries). Such persons could become citizens if they registered with the diplomatic or consular representative of India in that country. Hence, Article 8 is not the correct option as it relates to the Indian diaspora.
  • Article 9: This is a negative provision stating that if any person voluntarily acquires the citizenship of any foreign State, they shall no longer be a citizen of India. 📌 Important Points

  • Under the Citizenship Act, 1955, citizenship can be acquired in five ways: Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalization, and Incorporation of Territory.

Which folk music form of Uttar Pradesh is traditionally sung during the monsoon season by women?

Lavani

Correct Option: B) Kajri
Explanation:

The correct answer is Kajri.

📌 Key Points

  • Kajri is one of the most popular folk music forms of Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, specifically associated with the monsoon season (the month of Sawan). It is traditionally sung by women as they express their feelings of longing for their husbands or lovers while swinging on trees during the rains. Hence, the option Kajri is the correct answer.
  • The origin of Kajri is believed to be in the Mirzapur district of Uttar Pradesh. According to local folklore, it is named after Kajli, a woman who sang songs of separation while waiting for her husband.
  • It is often performed in two styles: Dhunmuniya Kajri, which is sung in a circle without any musical instruments, and Baithaki Kajri, which is a more formal rendition performed in a seated gathering with instruments like the Harmonium and Tabla.
  • The themes of Kajri usually revolve around the beauty of the rainy season, the dark clouds, the greenery, and the divine love stories of Radha and Lord Krishna.
  • The language used in these songs is predominantly Bhojpuri, Maithili, or Awadhi, reflecting the local culture of the Purvanchal region.

📌 Additional Information

  • Lavani:
    • This is a traditional folk song and dance form from the state of Maharashtra.
    • It is characterized by a powerful rhythm provided by the Dholki and is usually performed by women wearing nine-yard long saris (Nauvari).
    • It covers various topics like politics, religion, and romance, and was traditionally used to entertain soldiers during the 18th and 19th centuries.
  • Bhatiali:
    • This is a form of folk music from West Bengal and Bangladesh.
    • It is traditionally sung by boatmen (malla) while rowing down the rivers of the Ganges delta.
    • The word is derived from 'Bhata', meaning ebb or downstream, as these songs are sung while going with the flow of the river.
  • Maand (Maang):
    • This is a sophisticated style of folk singing from Rajasthan.
    • It is neither purely classical nor purely folk but sits somewhere in between. It is famous for the song 'Kesariya Balam', which welcomes guests to the state.
    • It was traditionally patronized by the Rajput royalty and developed in the Bikaner and Jaisalmer regions.

📌 Important Points

  • Semi-Classical Integration: Kajri has been adopted into Indian Classical Music, particularly by the Banaras Gharana. Legendary singers like Girija Devi, known as the 'Queen of Thumri', were instrumental in popularizing Kajri on global stages.
  • Geographical Identity: While it is the state folk song of Uttar Pradesh, it is also celebrated during the Kajli Teej festival in Varanasi and Mirzapur, where large-scale competitions are held.
  • Cultural Impact: Other notable folk forms of Uttar Pradesh include Rasiya (Braj region), Chaiti (sung in the month of Chaitra), and Birha (narrative ballads usually sung by the Ahir community).
  • Linguistic Diversity: The Kajri songs often use Braj Bhasha and Bhojpuri dialects, making them an essential part of the Indo-Gangetic cultural heritage.

A)
5
B)
8  ✓ (Correct Answer)
C)
7
D)
6
Correct Option: B) 8
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Total Volume = Cube Vol + Cuboid Vol = 1.5 × Cube Vol

⇒ Cuboid Vol = 0.5 × Cube Vol

Since both share the same base area (x²), their volume ratio equals their height ratio.

Cuboid Height = 0.5 × Cube Edge ⇒ (x − 4) = 0.5x

0.5x = 4 → x = 8

∴ Answer = 8

Alternate Method

Given:

Edge of cube = x cm

Height of cuboid = (x − 4) cm

Base of cuboid = x cm × x cm (fits top of cube)

Total volume = 1.5 × x3

Formula used:

Volume of cube = (edge)3

Volume of cuboid = Base Area × Height

Calculations:

Volume of Solid = Volume of Cube + Volume of Cuboid

⇒ 1.5x3 = x3 + [x2 × (x − 4)]

⇒ 1.5x3 = x3 + x3 − 4x2

⇒ 1.5x3 = 2x3 − 4x2

⇒ 4x2 = 2x3 − 1.5x3

⇒ 4x2 = 0.5x3

⇒ 4 = 0.5x

⇒ x = 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8

∴ The correct answer is 8.

📌 Additional Information

Volume of Prisms

The volume of any prism (like a cube or cuboid) is calculated as Base Area × Vertical Height.

Diagonal of a Cube

The longest diagonal of a cube with edge length a is given by the formula d = √3 × a.

Surface Area of a Cuboid

Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl), where l, b, and h are length, breadth, and height respectively.

Combined Volume Rule

When one solid is placed over another, the total volume is simply the arithmetic sum of their individual volumes.

  • The transport in xylem is unidirectional, meaning the movement of substances occurs only in the upward direction, from the bottom to the top of the plant.
  • It provides mechanical strength to the plant body due to the presence of lignified cell walls in its components.
  • The xylem consists of four main types of cells: Tracheids, Vessels (also known as trachea), Xylem Parenchyma, and Xylem Fibres.
  • Except for xylem parenchyma, all other xylem elements are dead cells at maturity, which facilitates the efficient flow of water under high tension.
  • Tracheids and vessels are the main conducting elements; they are tube-like structures that allow water to move through the plant via capillary action and transpiration pull.

📌 Additional Information

  • Phloem:
    • Phloem is the other major vascular tissue responsible for the translocation of organic nutrients, especially sucrose (sugar), produced during photosynthesis.
    • Unlike xylem, the transport in phloem is bidirectional (multi-directional), moving food from the source (leaves) to the sink (roots, fruits, and storage organs).
    • It is composed of Sieve tubes, Companion cells, Phloem parenchyma, and Phloem fibres. Most of these cells are living at maturity.
  • Epidermis:
    • The Epidermis is the outermost layer of cells covering the entire surface of the plant, including roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and seeds.
    • Its primary function is protection against mechanical injury, water loss (via the cuticle), and pathogen entry.
    • In leaves, it contains specialized pores called stomata, which are regulated by guard cells for gas exchange and transpiration.
  • Cortex:
    • The Cortex is a region of ground tissue located between the epidermis and the vascular cylinder (stele) in roots and stems.
    • It is mostly composed of parenchyma cells and serves functions like storage of food (starch) and transport of water and minerals from the epidermis to the vascular center.
    • In some plants, the innermost layer of the cortex is differentiated into the endodermis, which regulates the flow of materials into the xylem.

📌 Important Points

  • Ascent of Sap: The upward movement of water and minerals through the xylem is scientifically termed the Ascent of Sap. This process is explained by the Cohesion-Tension Theory, proposed by Dixon and Jolly in 1894.

Combined Time Shortcut

If A and B together take T hours, and A takes (T + a) and B takes (T + b) hours individually, then T = √(a × b).

Negative Work (Outlet Pipes)

If an outlet pipe is present, its efficiency is subtracted from the total efficiency. Total rate = RateInlet − RateOutlet.

Capacity Calculation

Total Capacity = Efficiency × Time. If flow rate (e.g., liters/min) is given, Capacity = Flow Rate × Time taken to fill.

Work and Wages

Wages are always distributed in the ratio of the efficiency (work done) of the pipes or individuals involved.

  • No Court Fee: One of the major features of this system is that there is no court fee payable when a matter is filed in a Lok Adalat. If a matter pending in a court is referred to the Lok Adalat and is settled subsequently, the court fee originally paid is refunded to the parties.
  • Types of Cases: It deals with various matters including matrimonial disputes, labor disputes, land acquisition cases, bank recovery cases, and Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) cases.

  • E-Lok Adalat Evolution: Following the success in Chhattisgarh in 2020, other states like Rajasthan, Karnataka, and Delhi also adopted the E-Lok Adalat model to clear backlogs using video conferencing platforms and WhatsApp video calls.
  • Article 39A: The Constitution of India, under Article 39A, provides for free legal aid to the poor and weaker sections of the society and ensures justice for all, which is the foundational principle behind the Lok Adalat system.
