Question No. 2Test (Q.2)
Why is the Paika Rebellion of Odisha regarded as distinct among early anti-colonial uprisings in terms of the social background and traditional functions of its participants?
A)
It depended on support from foreign mercenary forces
B)
It was organised by large zamindars seeking revenue concessions
C)
It was driven mainly by urban craft guilds and traders
D)
It was led by hereditary militia with local administrative roles  ✓ (Correct Answer)
Correct Option: D) It was led by hereditary militia with local administrative roles
Explanation:

The correct answer is Led by hereditary militia with local administrative roles.

📌 Key Points

  • The Paika Rebellion of 1817 was an armed uprising against the British East India Company's rule in Odisha, primarily led by the Paikas, who were the traditional landed militia of the state.
  • The Paikas were distinct because they held hereditary rent-free lands (known as Paikan Jagirs) in exchange for their military and police services to the Gajapati rulers of Khurda.
  • During times of peace, these individuals performed administrative functions, such as maintaining law and order and policing within their localities, while during war, they served as the standing army.
  • The rebellion was sparked when the British, after conquering Odisha in 1803, began resuming (taking back) these rent-free lands, imposing heavy land revenues, and abolishing the cowrie currency system in favor of silver.
  • The uprising was led by Bakshi Jagabandhu Bidyadhar, the former Military Chief (Commander-in-Chief) of the King of Khurda, who had been dispossessed of his ancestral Killa Rorang estate by the British.
  • Statement A is incorrect because the movement was entirely indigenous and did not depend on foreign mercenary forces; it was a grassroots resistance involving local tribals and peasants.
  • Statement B is incorrect because while some dispossessed zamindars joined, the Paika Rebellion was a much broader socio-militaristic uprising rooted in the loss of hereditary rights rather than simple revenue concessions.
  • Statement C is incorrect because the rebellion was rural and agrarian-military in nature, centered in Khurda, Banapur, and Puri, rather than being driven by urban craft guilds or traders.
  • Statement D is correct because the Paikas represented a unique social class that combined peasantry with military and administrative responsibilities. Hence, the correct answer is option D.

📌 Additional Information

  • Historical Context: The British East India Company defeated the Marathas in 1803 to take control of Odisha. The Raja of Khurda, Mukunda Deva II, was eventually deposed, leading to the collapse of the traditional Paika system.
  • Economic Grievances: Apart from land issues, the British established a monopoly over salt, which caused the price of this essential commodity to skyrocket, further fueling anger among the common populace and tribals like the Khonds.
  • Timeline: The rebellion began in March 1817 when a group of 400 Khonds from Ganjam entered Khurda and joined forces with the Paikas. They attacked the Police Station and Government Treasury at Banapur.
  • Suppression: The British declared Martial Law in Khurda. By 1818, the active rebellion was mostly suppressed, though guerrilla warfare continued for a few years. Bakshi Jagabandhu finally surrendered in 1825 and lived as a pensioner in Cuttack until his death in 1829.
  • Recognition: Many historians and the Government of Odisha have advocated for the Paika Rebellion to be formally recognized as the First War of Indian Independence, predating the Revolt of 1857.

📌 Important Points

  • Social Structure: The Paikas were divided into three ranks: the Paharis (shield-bearers), the Banua (matchlockmen), and the Dhenkiyas (archers).
  • Geographical Spread: While it started in Khurda, the insurrection spread rapidly to Puri, Pipli, Cuttack, and Kanika, showing the widespread discontent against colonial administrative policies.
  • Administrative Impact: Following the rebellion, the British were forced to make concessions in revenue collection and land settlements in Odisha to prevent further unrest.
  • Commemoration: In 2017, the Government of India celebrated the 200th anniversary of the Paika Rebellion with various national-level events and monuments dedicated to the martyrs of the uprising.

  • Military Architecture: The fort was equipped with underground passages, granaries, and deep wells to sustain the population during long sieges, reflecting the constant threat of foreign invasions during the 14th century.

Correct Option: C) Because they are dangerous to wildlife
Explanation:

The correct answer is Because they are dangerous to wildlife.

📌 Key Points

  • The Supreme Court of India, in its order dated November 13, 2025, reiterated that mining activities in the vicinity of National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries are inherently hazardous to the ecological balance.
  • Mining operations involve blasting, excavation, and the use of heavy machinery, which result in significant noise pollution and ground vibrations that distress and displace wild animals. Hence, option C is correct.
  • Such activities lead to the destruction of natural habitats and the fragmentation of wildlife corridors, which are vital for the migration and survival of species.
  • Mining does not create sustainable water sources; instead, it often leads to the depletion of groundwater and the contamination of streams with toxic minerals and silt. Hence, option A is incorrect.
  • While some argue that mining can boost the local economy, the Supreme Court held that economic gains cannot override the "Precautionary Principle" regarding the environment. Hence, option D is incorrect.
  • The court noted that mining activities generally deter tourism in the long run by destroying the pristine nature and biodiversity of the region. Hence, option B is incorrect.

📌 Additional Information

  • Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZ):
    • These are areas within 10 kilometers around Protected Areas, National Parks, and Wildlife Sanctuaries notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC).
    • The Supreme Court has previously mandated a minimum 1 km ESZ for all protected areas to act as a "shock absorber" for the ecosystem.
    • In these zones, activities are classified into Prohibited (like commercial mining), Regulated, and Permitted categories.
  • Wildlife Protection Act, 1972:
    • This Act provides the legal framework for the protection of various species of wild animals and plants and the management of their habitats across India.
    • It prohibits the entry into or destruction of wildlife sanctuaries without proper authorization from the Chief Wildlife Warden.
  • Environment Protection Act, 1986:
    • This Act authorizes the Central Government to take measures to protect and improve the environment and set standards for pollution emissions.
    • It is the umbrella legislation under which ESZ notifications are issued to prevent industrial hazards near sensitive zones.

📌 Important Points

  • Constitutional Mandate:
    • Article 48A of the Directive Principles of State Policy mandates that the State shall endeavor to protect and improve the environment.
    • Article 51A(g) establishes the fundamental duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment, including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife.
  • Public Trust Doctrine:
    • The Supreme Court frequently applies this doctrine, which states that resources like air, water, and forests are held by the Government in trust for the free and unimpeded use of the public and for the protection of nature.
  • Impact of Dust:
    • Mining produces particulate matter (PM 2.5 and PM 10), which settles on leaves, hindering photosynthesis in forests and causing respiratory issues for wildlife.

Question No. 7Test (Q.7)
Which of the following scientists proposed the nuclear model of the atom based on the results of the gold foil experiment?
A)
J.J. Thomson
B)
Niels Bohr
C)
John Dalton
D)
Ernest Rutherford  ✓ (Correct Answer)
Correct Option: D) Ernest Rutherford
Explanation:

The correct answer is Ernest Rutherford.

📌 Key Points

  • In 1911, the British physicist Ernest Rutherford conducted the famous alpha-particle scattering experiment, also known as the Gold Foil Experiment, which fundamentally changed the understanding of atomic structure.
  • The experiment involved bombarding a very thin gold foil (approximately 1000 atoms thick) with fast-moving alpha particles, which are doubly-charged helium ions possessing a mass of 4u and high energy.
  • Rutherford observed that most alpha particles passed straight through the foil, some were deflected by small angles, and a very small fraction (about 1 in 12,000) rebounded almost 180 degrees.
  • From these observations, he concluded that the atom contains a very small, dense, and positively charged center called the nucleus, where nearly all the mass of the atom is concentrated.
  • He proposed the Nuclear Model of the Atom, stating that electrons revolve around the nucleus in well-defined circular paths, much like planets around the sun.

📌 Additional Information

  • J.J. Thomson:
    • In 1897, he discovered the electron using cathode ray tube experiments.
    • In 1904, he proposed the Plum Pudding Model, which suggested that an atom is a sphere of positive charge with negatively charged electrons embedded in it like raisins in a pudding.
  • John Dalton:
    • In 1808, he presented Dalton’s Atomic Theory, which stated that all matter is made of indivisible particles called atoms.
    • He theorized that atoms of the same element are identical in mass and chemical properties, forming the basis of modern chemistry.
  • Niels Bohr:
    • In 1913, he modified Rutherford's model to overcome the problem of atomic stability.
    • He proposed the Bohr Model, stating that electrons revolve in discrete orbits (fixed energy levels) called shells (K, L, M, N), and they do not radiate energy while in these orbits.
  • Gold Foil Selection:
    • Gold was chosen for the experiment because of its high malleability, allowing it to be hammered into an extremely thin sheet, which was necessary to observe the scattering of individual alpha particles.

📌 Important Points

  • Rutherford is widely regarded as the "Father of Nuclear Physics" for his discovery of the atomic nucleus.
  • A major drawback of the Rutherford model was that it could not explain the stability of the atom; according to classical electrodynamics, an accelerating electron should continuously lose energy and eventually spiral into the nucleus.
  • The discovery of the nucleus led to the later discovery of the proton, a term coined by Rutherford in 1920 to describe the hydrogen nucleus as a fundamental particle.

C)

Correct Option: C) Ins
Explanation:

The correct answer is Ins.

📌 Key Points

  • The Ins key, short for Insert, is a toggle key found on most computer keyboards that is primarily used to switch between Insert mode and Overwrite mode (also known as Overtype mode) during text entry.
  • In Insert mode, which is the default setting in most modern word processors like Microsoft Word and Google Docs, any new character typed is placed at the current cursor position, and the existing text to the right is shifted forward.
  • In Overwrite mode, the newly typed character replaces or "overwrites" the character currently situated to the right of the cursor, effectively deleting the old text as you type. Hence, the Ins key is the correct option for toggling these modes.
  • The Ins key is typically located in the control keys or navigation block of a standard QWERTY keyboard, usually situated above the Delete key and to the right of the Backslash key.
  • On many keyboards, the Insert function is also shared with the 0 (zero) key on the numeric keypad, which is activated when Num Lock is turned off.

📌 Additional Information

  • Tab Key (Option A):
    • The Tab key (short for Tabulator) is primarily used to advance the cursor to the next tab stop in a document or to move focus between fields in a User Interface (UI).
    • In word processing, it creates a specific indentation, usually 0.5 inches by default.
    • It does not toggle between text entry modes. Hence, statement A is incorrect.
  • Del Key (Option B):
    • The Del or Delete key is used to remove the character immediately following the cursor or to delete a selected object or file.
    • Unlike Backspace, which deletes to the left, Delete performs forward deletion.
    • While it removes text, it does not change the behavior of how new text is entered. Hence, statement B is incorrect.
  • F5 Key (Option D):
    • The F5 key is a function key that serves different purposes depending on the operating system or software being used.
    • In web browsers like Chrome or Edge, it is used to refresh or reload the current page.
    • In Microsoft PowerPoint, it starts a slideshow from the first slide, and in Excel, it opens the "Go To" dialog box. Hence, statement D is incorrect.

📌 Important Points

  • Visual Indicator: In many terminal-based systems or older applications, the cursor changes shape when Overwrite mode is active, often switching from a vertical line to a solid block or an underscore.
  • Modern Software Behavior: Many modern applications have disabled the Insert key toggle by default to prevent users from accidentally overwriting their work. For instance, in Microsoft Word, you may need to enable this feature via the Options menu before the key will function as a toggle.
  • Combination Shortcuts: The Insert key is also used in various shortcuts, such as Shift + Insert, which acts as the Paste command in many Windows environments, serving as an alternative to Ctrl + V.
  • Legacy Use: The concept of Overwrite mode dates back to mainframes and early MS-DOS editors, where overtyping was often the primary method of editing existing text strings due to limited processing power and simple interface designs.

  • Inertia is the inherent property of an object to resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line. In this case, it is specifically Inertia of Direction, which is the tendency of a body to maintain its direction of motion. Hence, statement A is correct.
  • When the car takes a sharp turn, the friction between the tires and the road provides the necessary centripetal force to change the car's direction. However, the passenger's body, due to inertia, tries to continue moving in its original straight-line path.
  • As the car moves towards the center of the curve, the passenger feels an outward pull (often perceived as centrifugal force), which is actually just the body resisting the change in velocity (direction).

    • Inertia of Motion: The inability of a body to change its state of uniform motion. Example: An athlete runs some distance before taking a long jump to gain momentum.
    • Inertia of Direction: The inability of a body to change its direction of motion. Example: The sparks produced during grinding of a stone fly off tangentially.
  • Mass as a Measure: The quantitative measure of inertia is mass. An object with a higher numerical value of mass (measured in kilograms) possesses greater inertia and requires more force to change its state.

The correct answer is Mixed economy.

📌 Key Points

  • After gaining independence in 1947, India adopted a Mixed Economy model. This system was designed to combine the merits of both Capitalism (market economy) and Socialism (planned economy) to suit the socio-economic conditions of a newly independent nation.
  • In a mixed economy, both the Public Sector (government-owned) and the Private Sector (individually owned) coexist and contribute to economic growth. The government handles strategic sectors like defense, atomic energy, and heavy infrastructure, while the private sector operates in consumer goods and services.
  • The Industrial Policy Resolution of 1948 was the first formal announcement that outlined the mixed economic path, followed by the more comprehensive Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956, which aimed at achieving a socialistic pattern of society.
  • The primary goal of this system was to ensure rapid industrialization while preventing the concentration of wealth in a few hands, thereby promoting social justice and equitable distribution of resources.
  • The Planning Commission, established in March 1950, played a central role in this system by formulating Five-Year Plans to allocate resources across different sectors of the economy.

📌 Additional Information

  • Capitalist Economy:
    • In this system, the means of production are privately owned. Decisions regarding production, investment, and distribution are determined by market forces (supply and demand) rather than government intervention.
    • India did not adopt pure capitalism because it was feared that a profit-driven motive alone would ignore the needs of the poor and lead to monopolies.
  • Socialist Economy:
    • This involves state ownership of all productive resources. Economic activities are centrally planned to meet the needs of society.
    • While Jawaharlal Nehru was deeply influenced by the Soviet Union (USSR) model, India rejected total socialism to maintain democratic values and allow for private initiative and property rights.
  • Command Economy:
    • Often associated with authoritarian socialist regimes, a command economy is one where the government has total control over prices and production levels.
    • India’s model differed as it allowed a regulated market and private entrepreneurship alongside state control.
  • Economic Reforms of 1991:
    • Due to a severe Balance of Payments crisis, India introduced Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization (LPG) reforms under Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh.
    • These reforms reduced government licensing (License Raj) and increased the role of the private sector, further evolving India's mixed economy toward a more market-oriented structure.

📌 Important Points

  • Constitutional Basis: The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) in the Constitution of India (specifically Articles 38 and 39) provide the mandate for the state to minimize inequalities in income and ensure that the ownership and control of material resources are distributed to subserve the common good.
  • P.C. Mahalanobis: He was a key statistician and the architect of the Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961). He emphasized the development of capital goods industries through the public sector to make India self-reliant.
  • Current Status: Since 2015, the NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission to move towards cooperative federalism and a more modern approach to the mixed economy, focusing on policy guidance rather than direct resource allocation.
