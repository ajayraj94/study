A woman travelled at a speed of 20 m/min for 90 min, and at a speed of 55 m/min for 60 min. Her average speed (in m/min) for the entire journey is:
A)
34  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
27
C)
40
D)
38
Correct Option: A) 34
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Time ratio = 90 : 60 = 3 : 2

⇒ Average speed = (20 × 3 + 55 × 2) ÷ (3 + 2)

⇒ = (60 + 110) ÷ 5 = 170 ÷ 5 = 34

∴ Answer = 34 m/min

Alternate Method

Given:

⇒ Speed1 = 20 m/min, Time1 = 90 min

⇒ Speed2 = 55 m/min, Time2 = 60 min

Formula:

⇒ Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time

Calculations:

⇒ Distance1 = 20 × 90 = 1800 m

⇒ Distance2 = 55 × 60 = 3300 m

⇒ Total Distance = 1800 + 3300 = 5100 m

⇒ Total Time = 90 + 60 = 150 min

⇒ Average Speed = 5100 ÷ 150 = 34 m/min

Final Answer: 34 m/min

📌 Additional Information

1. Average Speed (Different Times): When time is different, use weighted average → (v1t1 + v2t2) ÷ (t1 + t2)

2. Average Speed (Equal Distances): Use harmonic mean → 2v1v2 ÷ (v1 + v2)

3. Shortcut Concept: If time ratio is given, multiply speeds with time ratio directly

The Lilatilakam, a 14th-century treatise, played a significant role in establishing the classical status of which Indian language through its grammatical and poetic principles?

Correct Option: C) Malayalam
Explanation:

The correct answer is Malayalam.

📌 Key Points

  • Lilatilakam is a 14th-century treatise written in Sanskrit that deals with the grammar and poetics of Mani-pravalam, a literary style used in medieval Kerala.
  • The text is considered the earliest known grammatical work on the Malayalam language, providing crucial evidence for its evolution as a distinct identity from Tamil and Sanskrit.
  • It defines Mani-pravalam as the "union of Manipravalam (Ruby and Coral)," where Mani represents Sanskrit and Pravalam represents the regional language, Malayalam.
  • The treatise consists of eight Silpas (chapters) covering various aspects of linguistic structure, including parts of speech, syntax, and poetic figures of speech.
  • By standardizing grammatical principles and poetic rules, Lilatilakam laid the foundation for the recognition of Malayalam as a classical and sophisticated literary language.

📌 Additional Information

  • Classical Languages of India:
    • The Government of India officially grants "Classical Status" to languages that have high antiquity, a rich heritage of ancient literature, and an original literary tradition.
    • Tamil was the first language to be declared a classical language in 2004, followed by Sanskrit in 2005.
    • Kannada and Telugu were added to the list in 2008.
    • Malayalam was officially recognized as a classical language in 2013, largely due to its historical roots evidenced in texts like Lilatilakam and its Chera dynasty connections.
    • Odia became the sixth classical language in 2014. In 2024, the Union Cabinet approved five more: Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, and Bengali.
  • Kannada: An ancient Dravidian language primarily spoken in Karnataka, with its earliest literature dating back to the Kavirajamarga in the 9th century.
  • Tamil: One of the oldest surviving classical languages, with the Sangam literature dating back to 300 BCE to 300 CE.
  • Odia: Predominantly spoken in Odisha, it has a rich history of inscriptions and literature like the Mahabharata by Sarala Das in the 15th century.

📌 Important Points

  • The term Mani-pravalam literally translates to diamonds and corals, symbolizing the aesthetic blending of two different languages to create a unique literary form.
  • Lilatilakam is historically significant because it identifies the Malayalam of the Chera kingdom (specifically the Kerala region) as a language independent of Tamil by the 14th century.
  • The Malayalam alphabet is largely derived from the Grantha script, which was used for writing Sanskrit in South India.
  • The establishment of Malayalam as a classical language requires that its early texts, like the Ramacharitam and Lilatilakam, demonstrate a tradition spanning over 1500 to 2000 years.

Question No. 7Test (Q.7)
Which of the following scientists gave the concept of atomic mass?
A)
Ernest Rutherford
B)
J.J. Thomson
C)
John Dalton  ✓ (Correct Answer)
D)
Niels Bohr
Correct Option: C) John Dalton
Explanation:

The correct answer is John Dalton.

📌 Key Points

  • John Dalton was an English chemist, physicist, and meteorologist who is best known for introducing the Atomic Theory into chemistry in the year 1808.
  • He was the first scientist to provide a logical explanation for the atomic mass of elements. He proposed that every element consists of tiny, indivisible particles called atoms, and that all atoms of a specific element have a constant mass that distinguishes them from atoms of other elements.
  • In his seminal work, "A New System of Chemical Philosophy", Dalton published the first table of relative atomic weights. He used Hydrogen as the standard reference point, assigning it an atomic mass of 1, because it was the lightest element known at the time.
  • His theory stated that chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of atoms and that atoms combine in simple numerical ratios to form compounds. The concept of atomic mass was essential to explain the Law of Definite Proportions and the Law of Multiple Proportions.

📌 Additional Information

  • Ernest Rutherford (1871–1937):
    • A New Zealand-born British physicist who is widely regarded as the father of nuclear physics.
    • In 1911, he performed the famous Gold Foil Experiment (Alpha-particle scattering experiment), which led to the discovery of the atomic nucleus.
    • He postulated that the mass of an atom is concentrated in a tiny, positively charged nucleus at the center, rather than being distributed throughout the atom.
  • J.J. Thomson (1856–1940):
    • An English physicist who revolutionized the understanding of atomic structure by discovering the electron in 1897.
    • He used cathode ray tube experiments to show that atoms contain negatively charged subatomic particles.
    • He proposed the "Plum Pudding Model" of the atom, where the atom was envisioned as a sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded in it like raisins in a pudding.
  • Niels Bohr (1885–1962):
    • A Danish physicist who proposed the Bohr Model of the atom in 1913.
    • He improved upon Rutherford's model by suggesting that electrons move in fixed circular orbits or energy levels around the nucleus.
    • His work incorporated quantum theory to explain how electrons can have stable orbits and why atoms emit specific spectra of light.
  • Modern Concept of Atomic Mass:
    • Today, the Unified Atomic Mass Unit (u) is used, which is defined as 1/12th of the mass of an unbound neutral atom of Carbon-12 at its nuclear and electronic ground state.
    • The relative atomic mass is a dimensionless physical quantity, representing the ratio of the average mass of atoms of a chemical element in a given sample to the atomic mass constant.

📌 Important Points

  • While John Dalton correctly identified that atoms have mass, modern science has discovered isotopes—atoms of the same element that have different masses due to a different number of neutrons.
  • The Atomic Number (number of protons), which defines the identity of an element, was a later discovery (by Henry Moseley in 1913), whereas Dalton focused strictly on atomic weight.
  • Dalton's atomic theory provided the quantitative basis for chemistry, transforming it from a qualitative study into a precise mathematical science.

Which Himalayan region has greater altitudinal variations?
A)
Southern Himalayan region
B)
Northern Himalayan region
C)
Western Himalayan region
D)
Eastern Himalayan region  ✓ (Correct Answer)
Correct Option: D) Eastern Himalayan region
Explanation:

The correct answer is Eastern Himalayan region.

📌 Key Points

  • The Eastern Himalayan region is known for having greater altitudinal variations compared to the Western Himalayas. This is primarily because the mountains in the east rise very abruptly from the plains of Assam and West Bengal.
  • Due to the sudden rise in height, the vertical zonation of vegetation is much more compressed and varied, leading to a rapid transition from tropical forests at the foothills to alpine meadows at higher elevations.
  • The region includes the states of Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, and the hills of West Bengal, along with neighboring countries like Bhutan.
  • The proximity of the Eastern Himalayas to the Bay of Bengal ensures that it receives heavy monsoon rainfall, which further enhances the biological and climatic diversity across different altitudes.
  • In contrast, the Western Himalayas rise more gradually through a series of ranges like the Shiwaliks and Himachal, resulting in less dramatic altitudinal shifts over short horizontal distances.

📌 Additional Information

  • Western Himalayan Region:
    • This region spans across Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand.
    • It is characterized by a greater width, reaching up to 400 km in some areas, whereas the Eastern section is narrower (approx 150 km).
    • The Western Himalayas are generally drier, with precipitation occurring mostly in the winter due to Western Disturbances.
  • Vegetation and Biodiversity:
    • The Eastern Himalayas are recognized as a Global Biodiversity Hotspot. It is home to unique species such as the Red Panda and thousands of varieties of Orchids and Rhododendrons.
    • The Western Himalayas are dominated by temperate coniferous forests featuring trees like Deodar, Blue Pine, and Silver Fir.
  • Snowline Differences:
    • The snowline (the altitude above which snow remains all year) is higher in the Eastern Himalayas (around 4,400 to 4,500 meters) because of higher humidity and lower latitudes.
    • In the Western Himalayas, the snowline is lower, found at approximately 2,500 to 3,000 meters on the southern slopes.

📌 Important Points

  • Highest Peaks: The Eastern Himalayan region houses Kanchenjunga, the highest peak in India at 8,586 meters, located in Sikkim.
  • Rainfall Data: The Eastern Himalayas receive more than 200 cm of annual rainfall, making them significantly more humid than the Western counterparts which receive roughly 100 cm or less in many parts.
  • River Systems: The Brahmaputra is the primary river system of the Eastern Himalayas, while the Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, and Beas originate or flow through the Western Himalayas.
  • Geological Fact: The Himalayas are Young Fold Mountains, formed by the collision of the Indo-Australian Plate and the Eurasian Plate during the Cenozoic Era.

Explanation:

The correct answer is Ctrl + F.

📌 Key Points

  • Ctrl + F: This keyboard shortcut is the standard command for the "Find" function across almost all operating systems and applications. When a student presses this in a PDF reader (like Adobe Acrobat or a web browser), a search bar appears, allowing them to type and locate specific words or phrases within the document. Hence, this option is correct.
  • Ctrl + V: This shortcut is used for the Paste function. It allows a user to insert content that has been previously copied or cut into the current cursor location or selected area. It does not provide any search or location functionality. Hence, this option is incorrect.
  • Ctrl + Tab: This is a navigation shortcut used to switch between different tabs within a single window, such as in Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, or Microsoft Edge. It moves the focus to the next tab to the right but does not search for text. Hence, this option is incorrect.
  • Ctrl + C: This is the command for Copy. It places the selected text, image, or file into the system clipboard so it can be moved or duplicated elsewhere. While it helps in selecting text, it cannot search for words inside a PDF. Hence, this option is incorrect.

📌 Additional Information

  • PDF Navigation and Tools: Portable Document Format (PDF) files are widely used because they maintain formatting across different devices. To navigate them efficiently, shortcuts like Ctrl + G (Go to page) or Ctrl + Home (Go to start) are often used alongside Ctrl + F.
  • Cross-Platform Consistency: Keyboard shortcuts are designed for efficiency and ergonomics. In macOS, the equivalent of the Control (Ctrl) key is the Command (Cmd) key. Therefore, to find a word on a Mac, a user would press Cmd + F.
  • Advanced Search Features: Most PDF readers provide advanced "Find" options such as Case Sensitivity (matching capital letters exactly), Whole Words Only (to avoid finding parts of larger words), and Find Previous/Next using Enter or Shift + Enter.
  • History of Shortcuts: Many modern shortcuts were popularized by Xerox PARC and later adopted by Apple and Microsoft in the 1980s. The "F" in Ctrl + F stands for "Find", making it a mnemonic shortcut that is easy for users to remember.
  • Browser Integration: Since many students open PDFs directly in web browsers, Ctrl + F works identically in browsers like Chrome and Safari to highlight every instance of the searched term in the visible text.

📌 Important Points

  • Universal Shortcuts: Some of the most common Windows shortcuts include Ctrl + S for Save, Ctrl + P for Print, Ctrl + Z for Undo, and Ctrl + A for Select All. These are vital for digital literacy and productivity.
  • OCR Limitations: If a PDF is a scanned image rather than a text-based file, Ctrl + F might not work unless the file has undergone Optical Character Recognition (OCR). OCR technology converts images of typed text into machine-encoded text that can be searched.
  • Selection vs. Search: While Ctrl + C and Ctrl + V are part of the "Copy-Paste" workflow, they are independent of the "Search" workflow triggered by Ctrl + F.
  • Application Variety: In Excel, Ctrl + F opens a dialog box that allows searching across Worksheets or Workbooks, demonstrating its versatility beyond just PDFs.

Cheriyal scroll painting belongs to which State of India?

Explanation:

The correct answer is Telangana.

📌 Key Points

  • Cheriyal Scroll Painting is a highly stylized and unique version of Nakashi art, which is native to the state of Telangana in India.
  • This traditional art form originated in the Cheriyal village, located in the Siddipet district of Telangana, and has been practiced for several centuries.
  • The paintings are primarily used as a tool for storytelling. These scrolls serve as visual aids for traditional balladeers who travel from village to village, narrating stories from Indian Mythology, the Puranas, and the Epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata.
  • In recognition of its unique cultural value and specific geographical origin, Cheriyal Scroll Painting was awarded the Geographical Indication (GI) tag in the year 2007.
  • The canvas for these paintings is traditionally prepared using khadi cloth, which is treated with a specialized paste made from tamarind seeds, white mud, and rice starch to ensure the longevity and texture of the Patam (scroll).

📌 Additional Information

  • The artisans use natural colors extracted from various organic sources; for instance, white is obtained from ground sea shells, black from lamp soot, yellow from turmeric, and blue from the indigo plant.
  • A distinctive feature of Cheriyal paintings is the red background, which makes the figures and narratives stand out prominently. The characters are usually depicted in profile with sharp features and large, expressive eyes.
  • The Kaki Padagollu community is the primary group of storytellers associated with these scrolls, using them to perform folklore and mythological dramas for rural audiences.
  • Beyond the large scrolls, which could historically reach 40 to 45 feet in length, modern artisans also create Cheriyal masks and dolls. These are made from sawdust and tamarind paste and often represent various deities or characters from rural life.
  • Other notable GI-tagged crafts from Telangana include Pochampally Ikat, Silver Filigree of Karimnagar, Pembarthi Metal Craft, and Bidriware from Hyderabad.

📌 Important Points

  • While scroll paintings are found in other parts of India, such as Pattachitra in Odisha and Phad paintings in Rajasthan, the Cheriyal style is unique due to its specific iconography and the influence of Deccani artistic traditions.
  • In the past, each community had its own set of scrolls; for example, the Madiga community had Goud Purana scrolls, and the Mala community had Markandeya Purana scrolls, showing the deep link between the art and the social fabric of Telangana.
  • Due to modernization and the decline of traditional storytelling, the scrolls have been scaled down in size. Today, they are often produced as 1-foot to 3-foot panels intended for interior decoration and souvenirs to preserve the heritage.

Question No. 16Test (Q.16)
In the figure given, parallel lines, AB and CD, are intercepted by a transversal t at E and F, respectively. If EG is the angle bisector of ∠AEF, find ∠EGF.
A)
35&deg;
B)
60&deg;  ✓ (Correct Answer)
C)
85&deg;
D)
90&deg;
Correct Option: B) 60&deg;
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

∠AEF = Corresponding ∠CFt (external) = 70°.

∠GEF = ∠AEF ÷ 2 = 70° ÷ 2 = 35°.

∠EFG = 180° − (70° + 25°) = 85°.

∠EGF = 180° − (35° + 85°) = 180° − 120° = 60°.

∴ Answer = 60°

Alternate Method

Given:

AB || CD, Transversal t. EG bisects ∠AEF. ∠CFG = 25°, ∠CFt (lower angle) = 70°.

Formula used:

1. Corresponding angles are equal for parallel lines.

2. Linear pair angles sum to 180°.

3. Angle Sum Property of a triangle = 180°.

Calculations:

Step 1: Find ∠AEF.

⇒ ∠AEF and ∠CFt (70°) are corresponding angles.

⇒ ∠AEF = 70°

Step 2: Find ∠GEF.

⇒ EG is the bisector of ∠AEF.

⇒ ∠GEF = 70° ÷ 2 = 35°

Step 3: Find ∠EFG.

⇒ ∠CFE + ∠CFt = 180° (Linear pair)

⇒ ∠CFE = 180° − 70° = 110°

⇒ ∠EFG = ∠CFE − ∠CFG = 110° − 25° = 85°

Step 4: Find ∠EGF (x).

⇒ In ΔEGF, ∠EGF + ∠GEF + ∠EFG = 180°

⇒ x + 35° + 85° = 180°

⇒ x + 120° = 180°

⇒ x = 60°

∴ The correct answer is 60°.

📌 Additional Information

Alternative Interior Angles

When two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, alternative interior angles (like ∠AEF and ∠EFD) are equal.

Consecutive Interior Angles

Angles on the same side of the transversal between parallel lines (like ∠BEF and ∠EFD) sum to 180°.

Vertically Opposite Angles

Angles opposite each other at a vertex where two lines cross are always equal.

External Angle of Triangle

An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the two opposite interior angles.

  • A monohybrid cross is a genetic cross between two individuals that differ in only one trait or characteristic. This type of cross was famously used by Gregor Mendel, often referred to as the Father of Genetics, to understand the patterns of inheritance in 1856-1863.
  • The F2 generation (Second Filial generation) is produced by the self-pollination or selfing of the F1 generation (First Filial generation) plants. In Mendel's experiments with the garden pea (Pisum sativum), the F1 generation always displayed the dominant phenotype.
  • When the F1 hybrids (genotype Tt) were crossed with each other, the recessive trait that was hidden in the F1 generation reappeared in the F2 generation. The phenotypic ratio observed was 3:1, meaning 75% of the offspring exhibited the dominant trait and 25% exhibited the recessive trait.
  • The genotypic ratio of the F2 generation in a monohybrid cross is 1:2:1, consisting of one homozygous dominant (TT), two heterozygous (Tt), and one homozygous recessive (tt) individual.
  • This 3:1 ratio confirms the Law of Segregation, which states that allele pairs separate during gamete formation and randomly unite at fertilization. Hence, the statement regarding the 3:1 ratio is correct.

📌 Additional Information

  • Genotype vs. Phenotype: The genotype refers to the actual genetic makeup of an organism (the alleles it carries), while the phenotype refers to the observable physical features or functions resulting from those genes, such as height, color, or shape.

  • Dihybrid Cross: If two pairs of contrasting traits are studied simultaneously (e.g., seed shape and seed color), the F2 phenotypic ratio changes to 9:3:3:1. This led to Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment.
  • Punnett Square: This statistical tool was developed by Reginald C. Punnett in 1905 to predict the probability of offspring inheriting specific genotypes and phenotypes from a cross.
  • Mendel's Selection: Mendel chose 7 pairs of contrasting traits in pea plants, including stem height (Tall/Dwarf), flower position (Axial/Terminal), and seed color (Yellow/Green), because they were easily identifiable and the plants were easy to cultivate.

📌 Important Points
