Question No. 3Test (Q.3)
The Harappan civilisation primarily sourced its copper from which region?
A)
Karnataka
B)
Madhya Pradesh
C)
Rajasthan  ✓ (Correct Answer)
D)
Tamil Nadu
Correct Option: C) Rajasthan
Explanation:
The correct answer is Rajasthan.

📌 Key Points

  • The Harappan civilization, which flourished during the Bronze Age (approximately 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE), had a sophisticated network for sourcing raw materials. Copper was one of the most vital metals used for making tools, weapons, and ornaments.
  • Archaeological evidence and chemical analysis of artifacts indicate that the primary indigenous source of copper for the Harappans was the Khetri region located in present-day Rajasthan. Hence, the option Rajasthan is correct.
  • The Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture, located in the Khetri area, was a non-Harappan contemporary culture that likely supplied copper items and raw ore to the Harappan settlements. This region is still one of India’s largest copper-producing belts.
  • In addition to domestic sources, Harappans also imported copper from Oman, a region on the southeastern tip of the Arabian Peninsula, known in ancient Mesopotamian texts as Magan. Both Harappan copper and Omani copper contain traces of nickel, suggesting a common origin.
  • The procurement of copper was essential for the production of bronze, an alloy made by mixing copper with tin. This technological advancement allowed the Harappans to create durable implements for agriculture and urban construction.

📌 Additional Information

  • Karnataka (Gold Sourcing): While Rajasthan provided copper, the Harappans sourced gold primarily from South India, specifically the Kolar region in Karnataka. This indicates an extensive internal trade network spanning thousands of kilometers across the Indian subcontinent.
  • Tin Sourcing: To manufacture bronze, the Harappans required tin, which was a scarce commodity. It is believed that tin was imported from Afghanistan and Iran. The presence of Harappan trading outposts like Shortughai in Afghanistan supports this theory of long-distance resource procurement.
  • Lapis Lazuli and Precious Stones: The Harappans were known for their bead-making industry. They sourced Lapis Lazuli (a blue stone) from Shortughai in Afghanistan, Carnelian from Lothal and Bharuch in Gujarat, and Steatite from South Rajasthan and North Gujarat.
  • Shell and Marine Resources: Specialized centers like Nageshwar and Balakot were established near the coast to source shells for making bangles, ladles, and inlay work, showcasing their geographical specialization based on resource availability.
  • Trade with Mesopotamia: Records from the Akkadian Empire (circa 2350 BCE) mention trade with a land called Meluhha, which historians identify as the Indus Valley Civilization. They traded copper, gold, ivory, and timbers for Mesopotamian textiles and oils.

📌 Important Points

  • Metallurgical Skills: The Harappans were experts in smelting and casting. They used lost-wax casting (Cire Perdue) to create intricate bronze statues, such as the famous Dancing Girl found at Mohenjo-daro.
  • Distance and Logistics: The transport of heavy ores from Rajasthan to cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (located in modern-day Pakistan) was likely facilitated by bullock carts and riverine routes along the Indus and its tributaries.
  • Identification of Ores: Modern spectrographic analysis has confirmed that the chemical fingerprints of copper artifacts in Harappan sites align closely with the chalcopyrite ores found in the Aravalli Range of Rajasthan.
  • Social Impact: The control over copper and bronze resources likely contributed to the social stratification of Harappan society, where a ruling elite or merchant class managed the monopoly of trade and production of prestige goods.

Given:

Question No. 7Test (Q.7)
The power to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights is vested in_____ .
A)
Supreme Court and High Courts  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
Lok Adalat and High Courts
C)
District Court and High Courts
D)
Session court and High Courts
Correct Option: A) Supreme Court and High Courts
Explanation:
The correct answer is Supreme Court and High Courts.

📌 Key Points

  • The Supreme Court of India is empowered to issue writs under Article 32 of the Constitution of India for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights. This power is part of the Right to Constitutional Remedies, which Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described as the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution." Hence, the inclusion of the Supreme Court in the correct option is accurate.
  • The High Courts in various states are vested with the power to issue writs under Article 226 of the Constitution. Unlike the Supreme Court, High Courts can issue these writs not only for the protection of Fundamental Rights but also for "any other purpose," meaning they can enforce ordinary legal rights as well. Hence, the inclusion of High Courts in the correct option is accurate.
  • There are five types of writs recognized in the Indian legal system: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo-Warranto. These serve as extraordinary remedies to ensure that the executive and lower judiciaries act within the bounds of the law.
  • The writ jurisdiction of the High Court is technically wider than that of the Supreme Court because it covers both fundamental and legal rights, whereas the Supreme Court’s jurisdiction under Article 32 is specifically limited to Fundamental Rights (Part III of the Constitution).

📌 Additional Information

  • Types of Writs and their Functions:
    • Habeas Corpus: Literally meaning "to have the body," it is a safeguard against arbitrary detention. The court orders the person who has detained another to produce the body of the latter before it to examine the legality of the detention.
    • Mandamus: Meaning "we command," this writ is issued to a public official, public body, or lower court to perform a duty that they have failed to perform. It cannot be issued against the President of India or State Governors.
    • Prohibition: Issued by a higher court to a lower court or quasi-judicial body to prevent them from exceeding their jurisdiction or acting contrary to the rules of natural justice. It is issued while the proceedings are pending.
    • Certiorari: Meaning "to be certified," it is issued to quash an order already passed by a lower court or tribunal. While Prohibition is preventive, Certiorari is both preventive and curative.
    • Quo-Warranto: Meaning "by what authority," it is issued to enquire into the legality of the claim of a person to a public office. It prevents illegal usurpation of public office by a person.
  • Geographical and Functional Scope:
    • The Supreme Court can issue writs against a person or government throughout the territory of India.
    • A High Court can issue writs against a person or authority residing within its territorial jurisdiction or outside its jurisdiction if the cause of action arises within its territories.
    • The power of the Supreme Court to issue writs is a Fundamental Right (Article 32), meaning the court cannot refuse to exercise this power. Conversely, the power of the High Court under Article 226 is discretionary.

📌 Important Points

  • Historical Context: The writ system in India is borrowed from English Common Law, where they are known as Prerogative Writs. Before 1950, only the High Courts of Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras had the power to issue writs. With the commencement of the Constitution on 26th January 1950, this power was extended to all High Courts and the Supreme Court.
  • Article 33 and 34: The Parliament has the power to restrict or abrogate the application of Fundamental Rights (and consequently writ remedies) to members of the Armed Forces, paramilitary forces, and police forces to ensure proper discharge of duties.
  • Basic Structure Doctrine: In the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) and later in L. Chandra Kumar case (1997), the Supreme Court ruled that the power of judicial review and the writ jurisdiction of High Courts and the Supreme Court form a part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution and cannot be abolished even by a Constitutional Amendment.

Which states suffered 30-70% below-normal rainfall in August 2025, contributing to crop stress and water shortages?
A)
Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, UP, Punjab, and the Northeast  ✓ (Correct Answer)

Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Goa, and Gujarat

Rajasthan, Haryana, Sikkim, Assam, Manipur, and Tripura

Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh
Correct Option: A) Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, UP, Punjab, and the Northeast
Explanation:
The correct answer is Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, UP, Punjab, and the Northeast.

📌 Key Points

  • The regions of Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh (UP), Punjab, and several states in the Northeast experienced a significant rainfall deficit ranging between 30% and 70% during the month of August. This period is critical for the Southwest Monsoon cycle in India.
  • A rainfall deficit of this magnitude falls under the "Deficient" to "Large Deficient" categories as defined by the India Meteorological Department (IMD). In states like Bihar and UP, this lack of moisture during the peak monsoon month leads to severe crop stress, particularly for water-intensive crops.
  • The Indo-Gangetic plains, including Punjab and UP, rely heavily on August rainfall for the maturation of Kharif crops such as Paddy (Rice), Maize, and Sugarcane. A 30-70% drop in precipitation often necessitates the increased use of groundwater, leading to a drop in the water table.
  • In the Northeast and Arunachal Pradesh, such deficits are unusual given their high-intensity rainfall profile. This leads to water shortages for local hydroelectric power generation and affects the biodiversity of the Himalayan ecosystem.
  • The spatial distribution of the monsoon was highly uneven, with the North and East suffering while other parts of the country might have received normal or excess rain. This irregularity is often attributed to the El Nino phenomenon or the unfavorable position of the Monsoon Trough.

📌 Additional Information

  • India Meteorological Department (IMD):
    • Established in 1875, the IMD is the principal agency responsible for meteorological observations, weather forecasting, and seismology.
    • It categorizes rainfall as Normal (within 19% of the Long Period Average), Deficient (minus 20% to 59%), and Large Deficient (minus 60% to 99%).
  • Impact on Agriculture:
    • Kharif Season: This season lasts from June to October. Major crops include Rice, Soybean, and Cotton. Rainfall in August is vital for the grain-filling stage of Paddy.
    • Economic Consequences: Agriculture contributes approximately 18-20% to India's GDP. Substantial rainfall deficits can lead to food inflation and lower rural demand.
  • Geographical Factors:
    • The Monsoon Trough: A low-pressure area that extends from Rajasthan to the Bay of Bengal. If this trough remains south of its normal position, North and East India (like Bihar and UP) remain dry.
    • Break Monsoon: A condition where the monsoon trough shifts towards the Himalayan foothills, leading to a sharp decrease in rainfall over the plains while increasing it in the hills.

📌 Important Points

  • El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO): The warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean waters. It is historically linked to below-normal monsoon rainfall in India.
  • Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD): Often called the "Indian Nino," a Positive IOD can sometimes offset the negative impacts of El Nino, bringing better rains to the Southern Peninsula but not always to the Northeast.
  • State-Specific Data: During years of severe deficit, Bihar often records a departure of nearly 40-50%, significantly impacting its rain-fed farming systems. Ladakh, being a cold desert, suffers even more as its minimal water sources from glacier melt are not sufficiently supplemented by monsoon pulses.

  • The device was devised by the British geneticist Reginald Punnett in the year 1905. It provides a simple way to calculate the mathematical probability of inheriting a specific trait based on Mendelian inheritance laws.
  • The Punnett square works by placing the gametes (reproductive cells) of one parent across the top of a grid and the gametes of the other parent down the side. Each internal box of the square represents a possible zygote or offspring combination.
  • It allows biologists to distinguish between the genotype (the genetic makeup) and the phenotype (the observable physical characteristics) of the resulting offspring. This is essential because different genotypes can sometimes result in the same phenotype due to dominance.
  • The tool is primarily used for monohybrid crosses, which involve one trait controlled by a single gene, but it can be expanded into a 4x4 grid for dihybrid crosses involving two traits.

📌 Additional Information

  • Alleles and Variation: Alleles are different versions of a gene. An individual inherits two alleles for each gene, one from each parent. If the alleles are the same, the individual is homozygous; if they are different, they are heterozygous.
  • Genotypic and Phenotypic Ratios: In a typical Mendelian monohybrid cross between two heterozygous parents (e.g., Bb x Bb), the Punnett square reveals a genotypic ratio of 1:2:1 (one BB, two Bb, one bb) and a phenotypic ratio of 3:1 (three dominant traits to one recessive trait).
  • Dominant vs. Recessive: Dominant alleles (usually denoted by capital letters like B) mask the expression of recessive alleles (denoted by lowercase letters like b). A recessive trait only appears in the phenotype if the individual is homozygous recessive (bb).
  • Applications: Beyond basic biology, the Punnett square is used in selective breeding of livestock and crops to ensure desired traits are passed down. It is also utilized in genetic counseling to assess the risk of a child inheriting genetic disorders like cystic fibrosis or sickle cell anemia.
  • Limitations: While highly effective for simple Mendelian traits, the Punnett square is less useful for polygenic traits (controlled by many genes, like height) or complex inheritance patterns like incomplete dominance, codominance, and environmental influences.

📌 Important Points

  • .docx (Microsoft Word Open XML Document): This is a document file format used by Microsoft Word. It was introduced with the release of Microsoft Office 2007. These files are application-specific files created by users to store text, images, and formatting. They are not part of the Windows core system files. Hence, this statement is incorrect.
  • .zip (Compressed Archive): This is an archive file format that supports lossless data compression. A .zip file may contain one or more files or directories that have been compressed to save disk space. While it is a very common utility format used within Windows, it is classified as a utility or compressed file rather than a system file. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

📌 Additional Information

  • Windows System Files: These are essential files located typically in the C:\Windows\System32 folder. They are critical for the booting, functioning, and stability of the operating system. Deleting or altering these files can lead to a system crash or the Blue Screen of Death (BSOD).
  • Common System File Extensions:
    • .sys: These are system files that often contain device drivers and core hardware configurations.
    • .exe: While many are applications, core files like explorer.exe or winlogon.exe are vital system executables.
    • .ini: These are initialization files used to store configuration settings for the OS or specific programs.
    • .bat: Batch files are script files that contain a series of commands to be executed by the command-line interpreter.
  • Dynamic Link Library (DLL) Advantages: The use of DLLs helps in reducing the consumption of resources. When multiple programs use the same library of functions, the operating system loads only one copy of the DLL into the RAM, which is then shared across all active applications.
  • File Systems in Windows: Windows OS primarily uses NTFS (New Technology File System) for modern versions, which provides features like file permissions, encryption, and journaling to protect system files from corruption.

📌 Important Points

  • System File Checker (SFC): This is a utility in Windows that allows users to scan for and restore corruptions in Windows system files, including .dll files. The command used in the Command Prompt is sfc /scannow.
  • DLL Hell: This is a term used to describe compatibility issues that arise when different programs require different versions of the same .dll file. Microsoft addressed this in newer versions of Windows (like Windows 10 and Windows 11) using Side-by-Side (SxS) assemblies and the Windows Side-by-Side (WinSxS) folder.
  • Registry: The Windows Registry is a hierarchical database that stores low-level settings for the operating system and for applications that opt to use the registry. It often points to the location of various system files like .dll and .sys files during runtime.

Which of the following monsoon systems is also known as the retreating monsoon?
A)
Advancing Monsoon
B)
Western Disturbances
C)
Northeast Monsoon  ✓ (Correct Answer)

Correct Option: C) Northeast Monsoon
Explanation:

The correct answer is Northeast Monsoon.

📌 Key Points

  • The Northeast Monsoon is also known as the Retreating Monsoon because it marks the period when the monsoon winds start moving away from the northern parts of India towards the Indian Ocean.
  • This transition period occurs during the months of October and November. During this time, the Southwest Monsoon winds weaken as the low-pressure trough over the northern plains begins to move southward due to the apparent movement of the Sun towards the Equator.
  • The winds during this season blow from the land to the sea. As these winds cross the Bay of Bengal, they pick up moisture and bring significant rainfall to the Coromandel Coast, specifically affecting states like Tamil Nadu, Coastal Andhra Pradesh, and Puducherry.
  • This season is characterized by clear skies and a rise in temperature in the northern plains, a phenomenon often referred to as "October Heat". While the days are warm, the nights remain cool and pleasant.
  • The Northeast Monsoon is crucial for the agricultural practices in Southern India, providing the necessary water for Rabi crops in the region.

📌 Additional Information

  • Southwest Monsoon (Advancing Monsoon):
    • This is the primary monsoon season in India, lasting from June to September. It provides more than 75% of the country's total annual rainfall.
    • The winds blow from the sea to the land, originating from the Indian Ocean and splitting into two branches: the Arabian Sea Branch and the Bay of Bengal Branch.
    • It is essential for the Kharif crop season, which includes staples like Rice, Maize, and Cotton.
  • Western Disturbances:
    • These are extra-tropical storms that originate in the Mediterranean Sea region. They bring sudden winter rain to the northwestern parts of the Indian subcontinent.
    • They are highly beneficial for Rabi crops, particularly Wheat, in states like Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh.
    • They usually occur during the months of December, January, and February and are driven by the Westerly Jet Streams.
  • Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ):
    • The ITCZ is a low-pressure zone located near the Equator where the trade winds converge. The seasonal shift of this zone plays a major role in the onset and withdrawal of the Monsoon in India.
    • In July, the ITCZ moves to about 20°N-25°N latitudes, which is often called the Monsoon Trough.
  • Factors Influencing Monsoon:
    • The Differential Heating of land and water is the primary driver of monsoon winds.
    • Other factors include the presence of the Himalayas, the Tibetan Plateau, and global phenomena like El Niño and La Niña, which can significantly affect the intensity and timing of the rainfall.

📌 Important Points

  • Tamil Nadu receives the majority of its annual rainfall from the Northeast Monsoon rather than the Southwest Monsoon because it lies in the rain-shadow area of the Western Ghats during the summer months.
