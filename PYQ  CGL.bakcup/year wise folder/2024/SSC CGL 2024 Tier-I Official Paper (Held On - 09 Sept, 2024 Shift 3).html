📌 Key Points

  • The system of 'Subsidiary Alliance' was devised by Lord Wellesley.
  • Lord Wellesley was the British Governor-General of India from 1798 to 1805, and he introduced this diplomatic policy to secure British control over Indian states and ensure that they remained under British influence.
  • The system required Indian rulers to:
    • Maintain a British subsidiary force at their own expense.
    • Not engage in any foreign relations without British approval.
    • Provide territory as security for the maintenance of British forces.
  • This system was used effectively to expand British control over Indian territories by isolating rulers from one another and by exerting indirect control.

📌 Additional Information

  • Lord Canning (Governor-General from 1856 to 1862)
    • Lord Canning is best known for his role during the Indian Rebellion of 1857 (also called the Sepoy Mutiny).
    • He was the last Governor-General of the East India Company and the first Viceroy of British India after the British government took direct control from the East India Company in 1858.
    • Canning managed the rebellion and its aftermath, which led to the end of the East India Company’s rule and the establishment of direct British rule in India.
  • Lord William Bentinck (Governor-General from 1828 to 1835)
    • Lord William Bentinck is known for his reforms aimed at modernizing India under British rule.
    • He abolished Sati (the practice of widow burning) in 1829.
    • He also worked towards education reforms, including the promotion of Western-style education and the founding of English as the medium of instruction.
    • He is also credited with promoting the growth of the railways and civil services during his tenure.
  • Lord Dalhousie (Governor-General from 1848 to 1856)
    • Lord Dalhousie is known for his doctrine of lapse, a policy that allowed the British to annex any Indian state where the ruler died without a direct heir (adopted sons were not recognized as legitimate heirs).
    • This led to the annexation of several states, including Satara, Jhansi, and Nagpur. His policies caused resentment and contributed to the unrest that led to the Indian Rebellion of 1857.
    • He is also credited with the development of infrastructure, including the expansion of the railway network and the introduction of the telegraph and postal system.
