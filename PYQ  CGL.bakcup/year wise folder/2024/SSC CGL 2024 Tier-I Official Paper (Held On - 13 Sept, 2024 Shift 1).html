According to the question, after interchanging the numbers:

Option 1): 500 and 190

\(\frac{480 \div 0.3 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 27 \div 1.6} = 2 \) ⇒ \(\frac{480 \div 0.3 \times 5 + 190}{500 + 9 \times 27 \div 1.6} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{8000 + 190}{500 + 151.875} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{8190}{651.875} = 2 \)

⇒ 12.56 ≠ 2 (LHS ≠ RHS)

Option 2): 0.3 and 1.6

\(\frac{480 \div 0.3 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 27 \div 1.6} = 2 \) ⇒ \(\frac{480 \div 1.6 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 27 \div 0.3} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{300 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 90} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{1500 + 500}{190 + 810} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{2000}{1000} = 2 \)

⇒ 2 = 2 (LHS = RHS)

Option 3): 27 and 480

\(\frac{480 \div 0.3 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 27 \div 1.6} = 2 \) ⇒ \(\frac{27 \div 0.3 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 480 \div 1.6} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{90 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 300} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{450 + 500}{190 + 2700} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{950}{2890} = 2 \)

⇒ 0.32 ≠ 2 (LHS ≠ RHS)

Option 4): 5 and 9

\(\frac{480 \div 0.3 \times 5 + 500}{190 + 9 \times 27 \div 1.6} = 2 \) ⇒ \(\frac{480 \div 0.3 \times 9 + 500}{190 + 5 \times 27 \div 1.6} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{1600 \times 9 + 500}{190 + 5 \times 16.875} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{14400 + 500}{190 + 84.375} = 2 \)

⇒ \(\frac{14900}{274.375} = 2 \)

⇒ 54.30 ≠ 2 (LHS ≠ RHS)

Hence, "Option 2" is the correct answer.

Which of the following two quantities have the same dimensions?
A)
Work and torque  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
Power and moment of inertia
C)
Work and angular displacement
D)
Power and radius of circular motion
Correct Answer: A) Work and torque
Explanation:

The correct answer is Work and torque.

📌 Key Points

  • Work and torque have the same dimensions, which is energy or force times distance.
  • The dimension of work is given by M1L2T-2.
  • The dimension of torque is also M1L2T-2.
  • Both quantities are measured in Joules (in SI units) or Newton-meters.

📌 Additional Information

  • Work:
    • Work is defined as the transfer of energy that occurs when a force is applied over a distance.
    • In the SI system, work is measured in Joules (J), where 1 Joule is equal to 1 Newton-meter.
    • Work can be calculated using the formula: Work = Force × Distance × cos(θ), where θ is the angle between the force and the direction of displacement.
  • Torque:
    • Torque is a measure of the rotational force applied to an object.
    • It is also known as the moment of force.
    • The formula for torque is: Torque = Force × Distance, where distance is the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation to the line of action of the force.
    • In the SI system, torque is measured in Newton-meters (Nm).
  • Power:
    • Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.
    • The SI unit of power is the Watt (W), where 1 Watt is equal to 1 Joule per second.
    • Power can be calculated using the formula: Power = Work / Time.
  • Moment of Inertia:
    • Moment of inertia is a measure of an object's resistance to changes in its rotation.
    • It depends on the mass distribution of the object relative to the axis of rotation.
    • The SI unit for moment of inertia is kg·m².
  • Angular Displacement:
    • Angular displacement is the angle through which an object moves on a circular path.
    • It is measured in radians (rad).
    • Angular displacement can be calculated using the formula: θ = s/r, where s is the arc length and r is the radius of the circular path.
  • Radius of Circular Motion:
    • The radius of circular motion is the distance from the center of the circle to any point on the circumference.
    • It is a constant for a given circular path.
    • The SI unit of radius is meters (m).
