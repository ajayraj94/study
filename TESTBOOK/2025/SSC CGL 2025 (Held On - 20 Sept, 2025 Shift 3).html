⇒ 16 ÷ 4 × 8 − 3 + 2 = 31

Which Indian satellite is used for weather forecasting?
A)
Aryabhata
B)
INSAT - 3D  ✓ (Correct Answer)

Mangalyaan
Correct Option: B) INSAT - 3D
Explanation:

The correct answer is INSAT - 3D.

📌 Key Points

  • INSAT-3D is a dedicated meteorological satellite developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) for weather forecasting and disaster warning services.
  • Launched on 26th July 2013 by the Ariane-5 VA-214 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana, it is equipped with advanced weather monitoring instruments.
  • The satellite is fitted with a 6-channel imager and a 19-channel sounder to provide detailed information on atmospheric conditions such as temperature, humidity, and cloud patterns.
  • INSAT-3D enables accurate cyclone prediction, rainfall estimation, and fog detection, making it a cornerstone of India's meteorological services.
  • The satellite operates in the geostationary orbit and provides continuous coverage over the Indian Ocean region, enhancing disaster preparedness and response systems.

📌 Additional Information

  • Aryabhata:
    • Launched on 19th April 1975, Aryabhata was India's first satellite developed by ISRO.
    • It was primarily designed for conducting experiments in space science and communication technology.
    • Aryabhata was not equipped for weather forecasting and was focused on scientific research.
  • Cartosat:
    • The Cartosat series comprises remote sensing satellites designed for earth observation, especially for cartographic applications.
    • It provides high-resolution imagery for urban planning, infrastructure development, and disaster management.
    • While essential for geographical mapping, it does not perform weather forecasting tasks.
  • Mangalyaan:
    • Also known as the Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), this satellite was launched on 5th November 2013 by ISRO.
    • Mangalyaan focuses on exploring Mars and studying its atmosphere and surface, not weather forecasting.
    • It was a landmark mission, making India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit and the first nation globally to achieve this feat in its maiden attempt.
  • INSAT Series:
    • The INSAT (Indian National Satellite System) series is a group of multipurpose geostationary satellites developed by ISRO.
    • These satellites are employed for telecommunications, television broadcasting, meteorology, and disaster warning services.
    • INSAT-3D and its successor, INSAT-3DR, are specifically focused on weather monitoring and climate studies.
  • Key Features of INSAT-3D:
    • It provides day and night imaging capabilities for weather monitoring.
    • The 19-channel sounder helps in atmospheric profiling, providing data on temperature and humidity at different altitudes.
    • INSAT-3D is also equipped with a Data Relay Transponder (DRT) for data collection from unattended meteorological platforms and a Satellite Aided Search and Rescue (SAS&R) transponder for locating distress signals.

📌 Key Points

  • The Suez Canal is a crucial maritime trade route located in Egypt, connecting the Mediterranean Sea with the Red Sea. It allows ships to pass between Europe and Asia without navigating around the southern tip of Africa (Cape of Good Hope).
  • The canal plays a pivotal role in the global shipping industry, facilitating the transit of significant volumes of cargo, including oil, natural gas, and goods.
  • It is one of the most strategic waterways in the Indian Ocean region, enabling quicker transportation of goods and reducing shipping costs significantly.
  • The Suez Canal is approximately 193 km long, and its importance has grown due to its ability to handle some of the world's largest ships, including container ships and oil tankers.
  • It is operated by the Suez Canal Authority and contributes significantly to Egypt's economy through transit fees.

📌 Additional Information

  • Panama Canal:
    • The Panama Canal is located in Central America and connects the Atlantic Ocean with the Pacific Ocean. It does not pass through the Indian Ocean.
    • It is approximately 82 km long and plays a key role in facilitating maritime trade between the Americas and Asia.
  • Bering Strait:
    • The Bering Strait is a narrow passage located between Russia and Alaska, connecting the Arctic Ocean with the Bering Sea. It is not connected to the Indian Ocean.
    • This strait is significant for Arctic navigation but is not a major global trade route.
  • Drake Passage:
    • The Drake Passage is a waterway connecting the Atlantic Ocean and the Pacific Ocean, located between South America and Antarctica. It does not pass through the Indian Ocean.
    • It is known for its rough seas and is primarily used for scientific expeditions and Antarctic exploration.
  • Importance of Indian Ocean Trade Routes:
    • The Indian Ocean serves as a hub for global maritime trade, with critical routes connecting Asia, Africa, and Europe.
    • Major chokepoints in the Indian Ocean include the Strait of Malacca, Strait of Hormuz, and the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait.
    • Approximately 80% of global oil trade passes through the Indian Ocean, highlighting its strategic importance.

📌 Important Points

  • The Suez Canal significantly reduces the distance between major ports. For example, the route between London and Mumbai is reduced by approximately 7,000 km compared to the route around the Cape of Good Hope.
  • The canal is a vital link in the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) promoted by China, emphasizing its role in global geopolitics.
  • In 2021, the blockage of the Ever Given ship in the Suez Canal disrupted global trade, underscoring its importance in international shipping.
  • Key commodities transported through the canal include crude oil, LNG, coal, fertilizers, and consumer goods.
  • The canal contributes billions of dollars annually to Egypt's economy, making it a critical asset for the country.

📌 Additional Information

B)

Explanation:

The correct answer is Appears stationary.

📌 Key Points

  • A satellite with a 24-hour orbital period is in a geostationary orbit if it orbits above the Earth's equator and follows the direction of Earth's rotation.
  • Such satellites are positioned at an altitude of approximately 35,786 km (22,236 miles) from Earth's surface, ensuring that the satellite's orbital speed matches Earth's rotational speed.
  • From an observer on Earth's surface, the satellite appears stationary in the sky because it maintains a fixed position relative to a specific point on Earth. Hence, the satellite appears stationary.
  • This phenomenon is essential for communication satellites and weather satellites, as it allows continuous coverage of a specific area on Earth without the need for tracking moving satellites.
  • The concept of geostationary orbit was first proposed by Arthur C. Clarke in 1945, and it has since become a cornerstone in satellite technology.

📌 Additional Information

  • Geostationary Orbit (GEO):
    • A geostationary orbit is a circular orbit in the Earth's equatorial plane at an altitude where the orbital period is exactly 24 hours.
    • It is also known as a Clarke Orbit, named after Arthur C. Clarke, who first proposed its use for communication satellites.
    • This orbit is particularly useful for satellites that provide television broadcasting, weather monitoring, and communication services.
  • Orbital Mechanics:
    • The satellite must satisfy the condition of orbital resonance, meaning the satellite's orbital period matches Earth's rotational period.
    • The force of gravity and the satellite's centrifugal force are balanced at this altitude, ensuring a stable orbit.
    • A satellite in this orbit travels at a speed of approximately 3.07 km/s (11,068 km/h).
  • Applications of Geostationary Satellites:
    • Communication Satellites: Examples include INSAT series in India, which provide telecommunication, television, and meteorological services.
    • Weather Satellites: These monitor weather patterns and provide real-time data for forecasts, such as NOAA's GOES satellites.
    • Navigation Satellites: Some navigation systems use satellites in geostationary orbits to provide accurate positional data.
  • Key Characteristics of Geostationary Orbits:
    • The orbit lies directly above the Earth's equator (zero-degree latitude).
    • The satellite's orbit is circular, not elliptical.
    • These satellites always appear stationary relative to a fixed location on Earth's surface, which simplifies ground-based antenna design.
    • Geostationary satellites provide coverage to approximately one-third of the Earth's surface.

📌 Important Points

  • Low Earth Orbit (LEO) vs. Geostationary Orbit:
    • Satellites in LEO orbit at altitudes ranging between 180-2,000 km and complete multiple orbits per day.
    • Satellites in GEO remain fixed above one spot on Earth's equator, orbiting at a much higher altitude.
    • LEO satellites are commonly used for Earth observation, while GEO satellites are ideal for communication and weather monitoring.
  • Other Satellite Orbits:
    • Medium Earth Orbit (MEO): These satellites orbit at altitudes between 2,000-35,786 km and are used for navigation systems like GPS.
    • Polar Orbit: Satellites in this orbit pass over Earth's poles, providing global coverage, often used for Earth observation.
  • Example of a Geostationary Satellite:
    • The GSAT series developed by ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation) is an example of geostationary satellites used for communication purposes.
    • GSAT-30, launched in 2020, is a notable addition to India's geostationary satellite fleet.

How is revenue deficit calculated?
A)
Revenue expenditure - Revenue receipts  ✓ (Correct Answer)

D)

Correct Option: A) Revenue expenditure - Revenue receipts
Explanation:

The correct answer is Revenue expenditure - Revenue receipts.

📌 Key Points

  • Revenue Deficit is the difference between the Revenue Expenditure and the Revenue Receipts of the government. It indicates the shortfall in revenue receipts to meet revenue expenditures.
  • Revenue Deficit is calculated using the formula: Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure - Revenue Receipts.
  • A high Revenue Deficit implies that the government needs to borrow or generate funds from other sources to meet its operational costs, such as salaries, pensions, and interest payments.
  • Revenue Receipts include tax revenues (such as income tax, GST, etc.) and non-tax revenues (such as dividends, interest receipts, etc.).
  • Revenue Expenditure includes expenses incurred on day-to-day operations like salaries, subsidies, and interest payments, which do not result in the creation of assets.
  • Hence, the correct formula for calculating Revenue Deficit is Revenue Expenditure - Revenue Receipts.

📌 Additional Information

  • Types of Deficits in Public Finance:
    • Fiscal Deficit: It is the difference between the government's total expenditure and its total receipts (excluding borrowings). Formula: Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure - Total Receipts (excluding borrowings).
    • Primary Deficit: It is the difference between the fiscal deficit and interest payments. Formula: Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit - Interest Payments.
    • Budget Deficit: It is the difference between total expenditure and total receipts of the government, including both revenue and capital accounts. Formula: Budget Deficit = Total Expenditure - Total Receipts.
  • Implications of Revenue Deficit:
    • High Revenue Deficit indicates that the government is borrowing to fund regular expenses rather than investing in capital assets, which may not be sustainable in the long run.
    • It reflects the government's inability to meet its operational costs through its revenue receipts, leading to increased dependence on loans or disinvestment.
    • A persistent Revenue Deficit can lead to higher fiscal deficits and increased debt levels.
  • Steps to Reduce Revenue Deficit:
    • Improving tax compliance and broadening the tax base to increase Revenue Receipts.
    • Rationalizing subsidies and controlling unnecessary government expenditure.
    • Promoting disinvestment and monetization of government assets to generate additional revenue.
  • Examples:
    • In the Union Budget 2023-24, the government aimed to reduce the fiscal deficit to 5.9% of GDP, which included measures to control the Revenue Deficit.
    • Revenue Deficit in India for 2022-23 was targeted at 3.8% of GDP, reflecting the government's focus on reducing revenue expenditures and increasing revenue collections.

📌 Important Points

  • Revenue Expenditure: It refers to expenditures that do not result in the creation of assets, such as salaries, pensions, interest payments, and subsidies.
  • Revenue Receipts: These are the earnings of the government from tax and non-tax sources, excluding borrowings.
  • Balanced Budget: A budget is considered balanced when Revenue Receipts = Revenue Expenditure, leading to no Revenue Deficit.
  • Constitutional Provisions: Article 112 of the Indian Constitution mandates the presentation of the Annual Financial Statement (Budget), which includes details of Revenue Deficit, Fiscal Deficit, and other financial indicators.
