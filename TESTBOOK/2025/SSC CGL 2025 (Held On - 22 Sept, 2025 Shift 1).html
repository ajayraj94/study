Chandrayaan-1

Explanation:

The correct answer is Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission).

📌 Key Points

  • Mangalyaan, also known as the Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), was launched by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) on 5 November 2013.
  • This mission made India the first Asian nation to reach Martian orbit and the fourth space agency globally to do so after NASA, the Soviet space program, and the European Space Agency.
  • The spacecraft was launched aboard the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV-C25) from the Sriharikota spaceport.
  • Mangalyaan was designed to study the Martian surface, morphology, atmosphere, and mineralogy, contributing significantly to Mars exploration.
  • The mission was executed with a remarkably low budget of approximately ₹450 crores, making it one of the most cost-effective interplanetary missions globally.
  • This achievement demonstrated India’s technological capabilities in deep space exploration and inspired future missions like Chandrayaan-2 and Aditya-L1.

📌 Additional Information

  • Chandrayaan-1:
    • Chandrayaan-1 was India’s first lunar probe, launched on 22 October 2008.
    • It discovered the presence of water molecules on the Moon’s surface, a landmark finding in space science.
    • The mission was instrumental in mapping the Moon’s surface composition and topography.
    • The spacecraft carried 11 payloads, including instruments from ISRO, NASA, ESA, and other international agencies.
  • Gaganyaan:
    • The Gaganyaan mission is India’s first human spaceflight program scheduled for launch by 2024.
    • The mission aims to send Indian astronauts, known as Vyomanauts, to low Earth orbit aboard an Indian-made spacecraft.
    • It includes orbital crew modules developed by ISRO and emphasizes human space exploration capabilities.
  • Aditya-L1:
    • Aditya-L1 is India’s first solar mission, launched by ISRO to study the Sun’s corona, solar emissions, and solar wind.
    • The spacecraft will be placed in a Lagrangian point (L1) between the Earth and the Sun for continuous observation.
    • This mission will provide valuable insights into space weather phenomena and their impact on Earth.
  • India’s Space Achievements:
    • India is among the leading countries in space exploration, with missions like Chandrayaan-2, RISAT, and GSAT satellites.
    • ISRO’s focus on cost-effective technology has garnered global recognition, including its commercial satellite launches via the PSLV.

📌 Important Points

  • ISRO’s Role:
    • ISRO has been pivotal in advancing India’s space capabilities, with missions like Mangalyaan, Chandrayaan, and Gaganyaan.
    • Its achievements demonstrate self-reliance in satellite technology and interplanetary exploration.
  • International Collaboration:
    • ISRO has collaborated with agencies like NASA, ESA, and Roscosmos for research and payload development.
    • Such partnerships enhance India’s scientific and technological expertise in space exploration.
  • Budget and Efficiency:
    • ISRO’s missions are known for their cost-effectiveness, making it a preferred option for commercial satellite launches.
    • Mangalyaan’s budget of ₹450 crores showcases India’s capability for efficient space mission execution.

  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP) measures the total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a specific time period.
  • The value of intermediate goods is excluded from GDP calculations to avoid double counting, which would otherwise inflate GDP figures.
  • Intermediate goods are those used as inputs in the production of other goods and services, such as raw materials or components.
  • Only the value of final goods and services, which are sold to the end-user, is included in GDP measurements.
  • For instance, if a car manufacturer buys steel to produce a car, the value of steel is not separately added to GDP, as it is already accounted for in the final price of the car.

📌 Additional Information

  • Components of GDP:
    • Consumer Spending: Includes household expenditures on goods and services like food, clothing, and entertainment.
    • Government Expenditure: Includes government spending on public services such as education, defense, and infrastructure.
    • Net Exports: Calculated as exports minus imports. A positive net export contributes to GDP, while a negative net export reduces it.
  • Why Intermediate Goods Are Excluded:
    • Including intermediate goods in GDP would lead to double counting, as their value is already embedded in the final goods.
    • This exclusion ensures an accurate and undistorted representation of a country's economic output.
  • Examples of Intermediate and Final Goods:
    • Intermediate Goods: Steel used in construction, flour used by bakeries to make bread.
    • Final Goods: Cars sold to consumers, bread sold at grocery stores.
  • Methods of GDP Calculation:
    • Production Approach: Calculates GDP based on the value added at each stage of production.
    • Income Approach: Summarizes all incomes earned in the economy, such as wages, rents, and profits.
    • Expenditure Approach: Totals all expenditures on final goods and services.

📌 Important Points

  • Consumer Spending: Largest component of GDP in many economies, reflecting the purchasing power and demand of households.
