Question No. 27General Awareness (Q.2)
The process of "coral bleaching" within the Indian Ocean is most directly linked to:
A)
Anthropogenically induced sea surface temperature increases  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
Increased deep-sea mining
C)
Increased levels of ocean acidification from river runoff
D)
The effects of increased Arctic ice melt
Correct Option: A) Anthropogenically induced sea surface temperature increases
Explanation:

The correct answer is Anthropogenically induced sea surface temperature increases.

📌 Key Points

  • Coral bleaching occurs when corals lose their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae), which provide them with energy through photosynthesis. This phenomenon is primarily triggered by stress due to elevated sea surface temperatures.
  • The primary cause of increased sea surface temperatures is anthropogenic climate change, driven by greenhouse gas emissions from human activities like burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes.
  • When sea temperatures rise beyond the tolerance level of corals (typically 1-2°C above the seasonal maximum), the corals expel their zooxanthellae, leading to a loss of color and energy sources, which can result in mass mortality.
  • Studies in the Indian Ocean have shown widespread coral bleaching events linked to El Niño events and long-term warming trends, both of which are exacerbated by anthropogenic influences.
  • This phenomenon poses a significant threat to the health of coral reefs, which are biodiversity hotspots and provide critical ecosystem services like coastal protection and fisheries support.

📌 Additional Information

  • Ocean Acidification:
    • Ocean acidification refers to the decrease in pH levels of seawater due to the absorption of excess atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO2).
    • While it negatively impacts coral growth by reducing the availability of calcium carbonate needed for reef-building, it is not the direct cause of coral bleaching.
  • Deep-Sea Mining:
    • Deep-sea mining involves extracting minerals from the ocean floor. While it poses threats to deep-sea ecosystems, it has no direct link to coral bleaching in shallow reefs.
  • Arctic Ice Melt:
    • Melting Arctic ice contributes to global sea level rise and ocean circulation changes but is indirectly linked to coral bleaching as a consequence of global warming.
  • Significance of Coral Reefs:
    • Coral reefs support around 25% of all marine life, making them critical for biodiversity.
    • They provide services such as coastal protection, food security, and tourism, contributing billions of dollars to global economies annually.
  • Global Efforts to Mitigate Coral Bleaching:
    • International initiatives like the Paris Agreement aim to limit global warming to below 2°C, reducing the risk of widespread coral bleaching.
    • Programs like the Coral Triangle Initiative focus on sustainable management and conservation of coral reefs in the Indian and Pacific Oceans.

📌 Important Points

  • Coral Bleaching Events:
    • The 2016 global bleaching event was one of the most severe, with extensive damage reported in the Great Barrier Reef and the Indian Ocean.
    • Bleaching events are becoming more frequent and severe due to persistent global warming trends.
  • Adaptation Strategies:
    • Restoration efforts include coral farming and the development of heat-tolerant coral species.
    • Marine protected areas (MPAs) are being established to safeguard coral reef ecosystems from human-induced pressures.

Alternate Method

Given:

Calculation:

⇒ Let O be the center of the circle and P be the point of tangency on the circle.

⇒ In geometry, the shortest distance from the center of a circle to any straight line drawn completely outside the circle is the perpendicular distance.

⇒ Since OP represents the radius and perfectly meets the tangent line at P without crossing it, OP is that shortest distance.

⇒ Therefore, the radius OP is perpendicular to the tangent line at the point of tangency.

A secant is a continuous straight line that cuts through and intersects a circle at two distinct points, whereas a tangent line merely grazes the circle, touching it at exactly one precise point.
