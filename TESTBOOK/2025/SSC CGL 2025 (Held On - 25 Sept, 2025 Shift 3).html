The practice in the Parliament where the demands for grants are put to a vote after the passage of the time limit for discussion is called_______.
A)
guillotine  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
cut motions
C)
motions
D)
point of order
Correct Option: A) guillotine
Explanation:

The correct answer is Guillotine.

📌 Key Points

  • The term Guillotine is used in parliamentary procedures to refer to the process where the discussion on demands for grants is curtailed or bypassed, and the demands are put to vote after the stipulated time limit has been reached.
  • This practice ensures that the Parliament can meet its deadlines for financial legislation without unnecessary delays.
  • It is primarily applied during the budget session of the Parliament when the discussion on the budget has to be completed within a specific timeframe.
  • The Speaker or Chairperson of the House applies the guillotine to speed up the process of voting on the remaining demands for grants without detailed discussions.
  • Once the guillotine is invoked, all remaining demands are voted upon in a single motion, saving valuable legislative time. This practice is essential for maintaining the fiscal schedule of the government.

📌 Additional Information

  • Cut Motions:
    • A cut motion is a parliamentary procedure allowing members of the opposition to question specific allocations in the budget.
    • There are three types of cut motions: Policy Cut, Economy Cut, and Token Cut.
    • For example, a Policy Cut motion seeks to express disapproval of the policy underlying the budget, while an Economy Cut motion focuses on reducing expenditure by a specific amount.
  • Motions:
    • Motions are formal proposals made by members of Parliament to discuss, approve, or disapprove policies, issues, or specific matters.
    • Examples include adjournment motions, no-confidence motions, and censure motions.
  • Point of Order:
    • A point of order is raised when a member believes that parliamentary rules or procedures are being violated during a debate.
    • The Speaker or Chairperson decides whether the point of order is admissible or not, ensuring that the proceedings remain in order.
  • Budget Process in India:
    • The Union Budget is presented by the Finance Minister in Parliament, detailing the government’s revenue and expenditure for the fiscal year.
    • The budget process includes stages such as the presentation of the budget, general discussion, scrutiny by departmental standing committees, and voting on demands for grants.
    • The application of the guillotine marks the end of the voting process in the Lok Sabha.

📌 Important Points

  • Purpose of Guillotine:
    • The guillotine ensures that the financial business of the government is not delayed, allowing funds to be released for government activities and programs.
    • It is a time management tool for the legislature to handle its workload efficiently.
  • Historical Context:
    • The term "guillotine" originates from the French Revolution, where it was used for a swift and decisive execution process. In parliamentary terms, it symbolizes the swift closure of discussions.
  • Constitutional Provisions:
    • The budget process in India is governed by Articles 112 to 117 of the Constitution.
    • Article 112 refers to the annual financial statement, while Article 113 deals with demands for grants.
