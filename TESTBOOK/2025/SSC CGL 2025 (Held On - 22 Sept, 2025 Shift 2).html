    • Sulh-i-Kul became a model for pluralistic governance in the Mughal Empire.

    • Introduced Din-i Ilahi, a syncretic religion blending elements from Hinduism, Islam, Jainism, and Christianity, emphasizing ethical living and tolerance.
  • Contrast with Earlier Mughal Policies:
    • Unlike some earlier rulers, Akbar did not enforce Islamic orthodoxy on his subjects.
  • Impact on Society:
    • Reduced religious conflicts and promoted loyalty among diverse communities, strengthening the Mughal state.

D)

Correct Option: C) Less than the mass of the reactants
Explanation:

The correct answer is Less than the mass of the reactants.

📌 Key Points

  • Nuclear fission is a process in which the nucleus of a heavy atom, such as uranium-235 or plutonium-239, splits into two smaller nuclei, along with the release of energy and free neutrons.
  • During nuclear fission, a small amount of the mass of the reactants (original atom and neutron) is converted into energy according to Einstein's mass-energy equivalence formula: E = mc², where E is energy, m is the mass lost, and c is the speed of light.
  • As a result, the total mass of the products (the resulting smaller nuclei and neutrons) is less than the mass of the reactants.
  • This difference in mass, referred to as the mass defect, is the source of the immense energy released during nuclear fission.
  • The energy released in nuclear fission is utilized in nuclear reactors and nuclear weapons.

📌 Additional Information

  • Energy Release in Nuclear Fission:
    • When a nucleus such as uranium-235 absorbs a neutron, it becomes unstable and splits into two smaller nuclei (fission fragments) like krypton-92 and barium-141, along with the release of 2-3 neutrons and energy.
    • The energy released is approximately 200 MeV per fission reaction, which is significantly higher than energy released in chemical reactions.
    • This energy is primarily in the form of kinetic energy of the fission fragments and is later converted into thermal energy in reactors.
  • Mass-Energy Equivalence:
    • The principle of mass-energy equivalence, given by Albert Einstein in 1905, states that mass can be converted into energy and vice versa.
    • The formula E = mc² explains how even a small mass defect results in a large amount of energy due to the large value of the speed of light (c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s).
  • Chain Reaction in Fission:
    • The neutrons released in fission can trigger further fission reactions in nearby uranium or plutonium nuclei, leading to a self-sustaining process known as a chain reaction.
    • In nuclear reactors, this chain reaction is controlled using materials like control rods (made of boron or cadmium) to regulate the number of neutrons.
  • Applications of Nuclear Fission:
    • Fission is used in nuclear power plants to generate electricity by converting the thermal energy produced into electrical energy.
    • It is also used in nuclear weapons, where uncontrolled chain reactions lead to massive explosions.
  • Fission vs. Fusion:
    • While fission involves splitting a heavy nucleus, nuclear fusion involves combining two light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) to form a heavier nucleus with the release of energy.
    • Fusion reactions power the Sun and stars, but achieving controlled fusion on Earth remains a challenge due to the extremely high temperatures and pressures required.

📌 Important Points

  • Fissionable Materials: The most commonly used fissionable materials are uranium-235 and plutonium-239.
  • Neutron Moderators: Materials like graphite or heavy water are used in reactors to slow down neutrons, making them more effective in causing fission.
  • Waste Management: Nuclear fission produces radioactive waste, which requires careful handling and long-term storage to prevent environmental contamination.
  • Global Usage: As of 2023, nuclear power contributes about 10% of the world's electricity production.
