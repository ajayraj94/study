Question No. 21General Intelligence and Reasoning (Q.21)
The length of a rectangle is increased by 10%, and its width is decreased by 10%. What is the net percentage change in the area of the rectangle?
A)
-1%  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
0%
C)
1%
D)
-2%
Correct Option: A) -1%
Explanation:

Given:

Length of a rectangle is increased by 10%.
Width of the rectangle is decreased by 10%.

Let the original length = 100 and width = 100.

Original area = 100 × 100 = 10000

New length = 100 + 10% of 100 = 110
New width = 100 − 10% of 100 = 90

New area = 110 × 90 = 9900

Change in area = 9900 − 10000 = −100

Percentage change = (−100 / 10000) × 100 = −1%

Hence, the correct answer is Option 1.

What makes the SSLV (Small Satellite Launch Vehicle) distinct from PSLV and GSLV?
A)
It uses a hybrid liquid-solid engine
B)
It is designed for on-demand, low-cost launches  ✓ (Correct Answer)
C)
It operates from a sea-based platform
D)
It can only launch foreign satellites
Correct Option: B) It is designed for on-demand, low-cost launches
Explanation:

The correct answer is It is designed for on-demand, low-cost launches.

📌 Key Points

  • Small Satellite Launch Vehicle (SSLV) is specifically designed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) for on-demand, low-cost launches of small satellites.
  • SSLV is tailored to cater to the growing demand for launching small satellites into low Earth orbit (LEO) on short notice, making it a cost-effective and efficient solution.
  • Unlike PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle) and GSLV (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle), which are designed for larger payloads and more complex missions, SSLV focuses on flexibility and quick deployment of payloads.
  • SSLV requires a smaller launch crew and simplified logistics compared to PSLV and GSLV, significantly reducing the turnaround time and operational costs.
  • Its compact design allows SSLV to be assembled and launched within a few days, aligning with the growing commercialization of space and the demands of satellite operators.

📌 Additional Information

  • Features of SSLV:
    • SSLV is designed to carry a payload of up to 500 kg to low Earth orbit (LEO) and 300 kg to Sun-synchronous orbit (SSO).
    • It uses a three-stage solid propulsion system combined with a liquid-fueled velocity trimming module for precise orbit insertion.
    • SSLV's modular and simplified design ensures that it can be manufactured and launched quickly, meeting the needs of satellite operators for rapid deployment.
  • Comparison with PSLV and GSLV:
    • PSLV: Known as the workhorse of ISRO, PSLV is capable of carrying payloads of up to 1,750 kg to Sun-synchronous polar orbits. It is used for more complex and heavier missions.
    • GSLV: Equipped with a cryogenic upper stage, GSLV is designed to carry payloads of up to 5,000 kg to geostationary transfer orbit (GTO), making it suitable for large communication satellites.
    • Unlike SSLV, both PSLV and GSLV require longer preparation times and are more expensive due to their advanced capabilities and higher payload capacities.
  • Applications of SSLV:
    • SSLV is ideal for launching Earth observation satellites, nanosatellites, and microsatellites for various applications like remote sensing, scientific research, and communication.
    • It supports the growing demand for commercial satellite launches and provides a viable solution for startups and small-scale satellite operators.
  • 📌 Important Points
    • SSLV is an essential component of ISRO's strategy to cater to the global small satellite market, projected to grow significantly in the coming years.
    • ISRO's maiden SSLV mission, SSLV-D1, was launched in 2022, marking a significant milestone in India's space capabilities.
    • SSLV's ability to conduct frequent and cost-effective launches aligns with the goals of the Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Center (IN-SPACe) to promote private sector participation in space activities.

📌 Key Points

  • The concept of the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (QUAD) was first proposed in 2007 by the then Prime Minister of Japan, Shinzo Abe.
  • The QUAD is a strategic dialogue between India, the United States, Japan, and Australia, aimed at promoting a free, open, and inclusive Indo-Pacific region.
  • The idea was initially discussed in the context of the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, where these four nations coordinated disaster relief efforts, but it formally took shape in 2007.
  • This initiative was driven by concerns over the rise of China's influence in the Indo-Pacific region and the need for collaborative efforts to ensure regional security and stability.
  • Although the QUAD lost momentum after its initial proposal in 2007, it was revived in 2017 due to growing geopolitical tensions and shared concerns about regional challenges.

📌 Additional Information

  • Structure and Objectives of QUAD:
    • The QUAD is not a formal military alliance but a strategic forum that focuses on dialogue and cooperation in areas such as maritime security, counter-terrorism, climate change, and infrastructure development.
    • It aims to promote a rules-based international order in the Indo-Pacific region.
  • Key Events in QUAD's History:
    • The first formal meeting of the QUAD took place in 2007 on the sidelines of the ASEAN Regional Forum in Manila.
    • The group was revived in 2017 during the East Asia Summit in the Philippines, reflecting renewed interest in countering regional challenges.
  • China's Response to QUAD:
    • China has often criticized the QUAD, viewing it as an attempt to contain its influence in the Indo-Pacific region.
    • Chinese officials have referred to the grouping as an "Asian NATO," though QUAD members have clarified that the forum is not a military alliance.
  • Recent Developments:
    • In 2021, the QUAD held its first-ever summit, attended virtually by the leaders of the four member nations, focusing on vaccine distribution, climate change, and emerging technologies.
    • The QUAD has also emphasized maritime security and freedom of navigation in the South China Sea.

📌 Important Points

  • 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami:
    • The collaboration between India, the US, Japan, and Australia during the 2004 tsunami relief efforts laid the foundation for future discussions on multilateral cooperation in the Indo-Pacific region.
  • Shinzo Abe's Vision:
    • In 2007, Japanese Prime Minister Shinzo Abe articulated the concept of a "confluence of the two seas," emphasizing the need for cooperation between the Pacific and Indian Oceans.
  • Key Challenges:
    • Despite its strategic importance, the QUAD faces challenges such as differing national interests among its members and China's growing assertiveness.

  • The primary goal of this scheme is to boost domestic manufacturing by providing financial incentives to companies for incremental production in specified sectors.
  • The scheme aims to attract both domestic and foreign investments to build a robust manufacturing ecosystem in India, particularly in sectors like electronics, pharmaceuticals, textiles, automobiles, and more.
  • The PLI scheme is designed to reduce import dependency, promote export growth, and make India a global manufacturing hub.
  • By focusing on sectors with high growth potential, the scheme also contributes to the generation of employment opportunities and the development of cutting-edge technologies within the country.

📌 Additional Information

  • Key Features of the PLI Scheme:
    • The PLI scheme provides financial incentives in the form of subsidies or cash benefits based on incremental sales or production of goods in specific sectors.
    • It is targeted at key sectors that are critical for India’s economic growth, such as mobile and electronic components, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, textiles, and more.
    • The scheme is implemented with a budgetary allocation from the government and has a time-bound framework to achieve its objectives.
  • Benefits of the Scheme:
    • Encourages the development of a self-reliant India (Aatmanirbhar Bharat) by reducing reliance on imports.
    • Supports the creation of a globally competitive manufacturing base in India, promoting export growth.
    • Helps in the development of advanced manufacturing technologies and innovation.
    • Leads to large-scale employment generation across various sectors, benefiting both skilled and unskilled workers.

    • Electronics Manufacturing: The scheme covers mobile phones, semiconductor components, and other electronics to make India a global manufacturing hub for these products.
    • Pharmaceuticals: PLI incentives support the production of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and key starting materials (KSMs) to strengthen the healthcare sector.
    • Automobile and Auto Components: Focuses on the production of advanced automotive technologies to promote green mobility and electric vehicles.
    • Textiles: Includes incentives for the production of technical textiles and man-made fiber-based products.
  • Global Context:
    • Many countries use similar schemes to promote domestic manufacturing, such as China’s Made in China 2025 initiative.
    • India’s PLI scheme is a strategic step to compete with such global initiatives and improve its share in the global manufacturing supply chain.

📌 Important Points

  • Launch Year: The PLI scheme was first introduced in the Union Budget 2020-21 and later expanded to cover multiple sectors.
  • Budget Allocation: The government has allocated INR 1.97 lakh crore over five years for the implementation of the scheme.
  • Targeted Outcomes: The scheme aims to increase India’s manufacturing GDP contribution to 25% by 2025 from the current 16-17%.
  • Monitoring and Implementation: The scheme is monitored by respective ministries and involves collaboration with industries to achieve its targets.
  • Global Recognition: The PLI scheme has been acknowledged as a transformative initiative to position India as a global manufacturing leader.

📌 Additional Information

  • Dravidian Architecture:

    • The key features include pyramidal towers (Vimanas), intricate carvings, and mandapas (pillared halls).
    • The Gopurams are characteristic of later Dravidian temples, serving as imposing gateways that dominate the skyline.

📌 Key Points

  • Beta-minus decay occurs when a neutron in the nucleus of an atom is transformed into a proton, an electron (beta particle), and an antineutrino.
  • As a result of this process, the atomic number of the nucleus increases by one since a neutron is converted into a proton. However, the mass number remains unchanged because the total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons) stays the same.
  • This type of decay is characteristic of nuclei that are neutron-rich, meaning they have an excess of neutrons compared to protons.
  • Beta-minus decay is represented by the equation: n → p + e⁻ + ν̅, where "n" is a neutron, "p" is a proton, "e⁻" is the emitted beta particle (electron), and "ν̅" is the antineutrino.
  • Example: In the beta-minus decay of Carbon-14, the process transforms Carbon-14 into Nitrogen-14: ₆C¹⁴ → ₇N¹⁴ + e⁻ + ν̅.

📌 Additional Information

  • Alpha decay:
    • In alpha decay, a nucleus emits an alpha particle, which consists of 2 protons and 2 neutrons (a helium nucleus).
    • As a result, the atomic number decreases by 2, and the mass number decreases by 4.
    • Example: Uranium-238 decays to Thorium-234: ₉₂U²³⁸ → ₉₀Th²³⁴ + α.
  • Beta-plus decay (Positron emission):
    • In beta-plus decay, a proton is converted into a neutron, a positron (β⁺), and a neutrino.
    • The atomic number decreases by one, but the mass number remains unchanged.
    • Example: Fluorine-18 decays to Oxygen-18: ₉F¹⁸ → ₈O¹⁸ + β⁺ + ν.
  • Gamma decay:
    • Gamma decay involves the emission of gamma rays (high-energy photons) from an excited nucleus to release excess energy.
    • There is no change in the atomic number or mass number of the nucleus during gamma decay.
    • Example: After alpha decay, the newly formed nucleus may emit gamma radiation to stabilize further.
  • Radioactive decay:
    • It is a natural process by which an unstable atomic nucleus loses energy by emitting particles or electromagnetic waves.
    • The three main types of radioactive decay are alpha decay, beta decay, and gamma decay.
    • Each type affects the nucleus differently in terms of atomic number and mass number.
