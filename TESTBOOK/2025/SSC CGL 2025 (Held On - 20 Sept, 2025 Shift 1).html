Question No. 27General Awareness (Q.2)
What is the main tower of a Draviḍa-style temple known as?
A)
Shikhara
B)
Vihāra
C)
Gopuram
D)
Vimāna  ✓ (Correct Answer)
Correct Option: D) Vimāna
Explanation:

The correct answer is Vimāna.

📌 Key Points

  • The Vimāna refers to the main sanctum tower of a Draviḍa-style temple, built directly above the garbhagriha (sanctum sanctorum).
  • It is a hallmark of South Indian temple architecture, especially in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, where the structure is pyramid-like and intricately carved.
  • The Vimāna symbolizes Mount Meru, considered the sacred abode of the gods in Hindu cosmology.
  • Unlike the Gopuram (gateway tower), the Vimāna is located over the central shrine and is often smaller but more ornately designed.
  • The famous example of a Vimāna is the Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, where the Vimāna stands at a height of 60 meters.

📌 Additional Information

  • Shikhara
    • The Shikhara is the tower or spire found in Nāgara-style temples, common in North India.
    • It is typically curvilinear in shape and rises vertically above the sanctum sanctorum (garbhagriha).
    • The Shikhara represents the sacred mountain and is often adorned with intricate carvings and decorative motifs.
    • Famous examples include the Shikharas of the Khajuraho Group of Monuments and the Konark Sun Temple.
  • Vihāra
    • Vihāra refers to a Buddhist monastery or dwelling place for monks.
    • It generally consists of a courtyard surrounded by small cells for the monks, with a central hall used for congregational prayers or teachings.
    • Vihāras were prominent during the Mauryan, Gupta, and post-Gupta periods in Indian history.
    • Examples include the rock-cut Vihāras at Ajanta and Ellora.
  • Gopuram
    • The Gopuram is the ornamental gateway tower of Draviḍa-style temples, usually located at the entrance of the temple complex.
    • It is a monumental structure adorned with intricate sculptures of deities, mythological scenes, and decorative elements.
    • Gopurams are typically larger and more elaborate than the Vimāna, often serving as the temple's most striking feature.
    • Famous examples include the towering Gopurams of the Meenakshi Temple in Madurai and the Ranganathaswamy Temple in Srirangam.
