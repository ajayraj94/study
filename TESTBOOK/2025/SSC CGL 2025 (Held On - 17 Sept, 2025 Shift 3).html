Question No. 20General Intelligence and Reasoning (Q.20)
If 6 machines complete a task in 10 days, how many days for 15 machines at same rate?
A)
4  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
6
C)

D)
8
Correct Option: A) 4
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Use the direct inverse relationship formula for constant work:

M1 × D1 = M2 × D2

Substitute the given values into the formula:

⇒ 6 × 10 = 15 × D2

⇒ D2 = 60 ÷ 15 = 4 days

∴ The correct answer is 4 days.

Alternate Method

Given:

Initial number of machines (M1) = 6

Initial number of days (D1) = 10 days

New number of machines (M2) = 15

Formula Used:

Total Work = Number of Machines × Days

Group Days Total Work Efficiency
6 machines 10 60 6
15 machines 4 15

Calculations:

⇒ Let the total work be the LCM of work components or the total machine-days required.

⇒ Total Work = 6 machines × 10 days = 60 units

⇒ Let the number of days required by 15 machines be D2.

⇒ 15 × D2 = 60

⇒ D2 = 60 ÷ 15

⇒ D2 = 4 days

∴ The correct answer is 4 days.

📌 Additional Information

Inverse Variation Rule

When work remains constant, efficiency is inversely proportional to the time taken. If the number of machines increases, the days required to complete the same work will decrease proportionately.

General MDH Formula

For complex work structures: (M1 × D1 × H1) ÷ W1 = (M2 × D2 × H2) ÷ W2, where M is resources, D is days, H is working hours, and W is work quantity.

Individual Machine Contribution

If machines operate at the same rate, each individual machine contributes exactly 1/N of the total combined output rate for N machines.

    • PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) ÷ (% Change in Price)
  • In the given question:
    • Percentage increase in price = 10%
    • Percentage decrease in quantity demanded = 20%

    • A change in price causes a proportionately smaller change in quantity demanded.
    • Example: Salt, medicines, and essential commodities.

    • The percentage change in quantity demanded equals the percentage change in price.
    • Total expenditure remains unchanged.
  • Perfectly Elastic Demand (PED = ∞):
    • Consumers buy any quantity at a particular price but none at a higher price.
    • The demand curve is perfectly horizontal.
  • Perfectly Inelastic Demand (PED = 0):
    • Quantity demanded remains unchanged regardless of price changes.
    • The demand curve is perfectly vertical.
