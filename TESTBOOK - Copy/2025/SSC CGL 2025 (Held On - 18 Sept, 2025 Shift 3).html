BODMAS Table:

Given equation: 40 ÷ 8 + 10 - 5 × 2 = 5

1. ÷ and ×

Given equation: 40 ÷ 8 + 10 - 5 × 2 = 5

After interchanging the signs we get;

⇒ 40 × 8 + 10 − 5 ÷ 2 = 5
⇒ 40 × 8 + 10 − 2.5 = 5
⇒ 320 + 10 − 2.5 = 5
⇒ 330 − 2.5 = 5
⇒ 327.5 ≠ 5
⇒ LHS ≠ RHS

2. + and -

Given equation: 40 ÷ 8 + 10 - 5 × 2 = 5

After interchanging the signs we get;

⇒ 40 ÷ 8 − 10 + 5 × 2 = 5

⇒ 5 − 10 + 5 × 2 = 5

⇒ 5 − 10 + 10 = 5

⇒ 15 − 10 = 5

⇒ 5 = 5

⇒ LHS = RHS

3. ÷ and +

Given equation: 40 ÷ 8 + 10 - 5 × 2 = 5

After interchanging the signs we get;

⇒ 40 + 8 ÷ 10 − 5 × 2 = 5

⇒ 40 + 0.8 − 5 × 2 = 5

⇒ 40 + 0.8 - 10 = 5

⇒ 40.8 - 10 = 5

⇒ 30.8 ≠ 5

⇒ LHS ≠ RHS

4. - and ×

Given equation: 40 ÷ 8 + 10 - 5 × 2 = 5

After interchanging the signs we get;

⇒ 40 ÷ 8 + 10 × 5 − 2 = 5

⇒ 5 + 10 × 5 − 2 = 5

⇒ 5 + 50 − 2 = 5

⇒ 55 − 2 = 5

⇒ 53 ≠ 5

⇒ LHS ≠ RHS

So, '+ and -' are the correct signs to swap.

The correct answer is Designs and builds launch vehicles.

📌 Key Points

  • The Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), located in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, is the primary center for the design and development of launch vehicles for the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).
  • VSSC is responsible for developing critical technologies for satellite launch vehicles, including the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) and Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV).
  • It is involved in the research and development of materials, propulsion systems, avionics, and aerospace structures required for launch vehicles.
  • The center plays a significant role in ensuring the success of India's space missions by creating reliable and efficient launch systems capable of placing satellites into various orbits.
  • Named after Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, the father of the Indian space program, VSSC has been pivotal in achieving India's self-reliance in space technology.

📌 Additional Information

  • Launch site for ISRO missions
    • ISRO's primary launch site is the Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC), located at Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh.
    • SDSC serves as the launchpad for various satellite launch vehicles, including PSLV, GSLV, and Gaganyaan missions.
    • Although VSSC is not a launch site, it works closely with SDSC to ensure the seamless integration and launch of vehicles developed at VSSC.
  • Trains Indian astronauts
    • Training Indian astronauts for human spaceflight missions is the responsibility of the Human Spaceflight Centre (HSFC), based in Bengaluru, Karnataka.
    • The Gaganyaan mission, India's first human spaceflight program, includes extensive astronaut training, which is conducted in collaboration with international space agencies and training centers.
    • VSSC is not directly involved in astronaut training but contributes to developing launch vehicles and technologies for human spaceflight.
  • Controls ISRO satellites
    • The control and management of ISRO satellites are handled by the ISRO Telemetry, Tracking, and Command Network (ISTRAC), headquartered in Bengaluru.
    • ISTRAC ensures the successful operation, navigation, and communication of satellites in orbit, including remote sensing and communication satellites.
    • VSSC focuses on launch vehicle development, not satellite control or operations.

📌 Key Points

  • Post-independence, many princely states aspired to maintain independent or autonomous status due to historical autonomy and privileges enjoyed under British rule.
  • Popular movements, protests, and strong public sentiment within princely states significantly influenced the rulers to accede to India. These movements reflected the will of the people to be part of a unified Indian nation.
  • The integration process was spearheaded by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and V.P. Menon, who utilized diplomacy and strategic measures to persuade rulers while considering the popular will of the people.
  • Many princely states, such as Hyderabad, Junagadh, and Kashmir, faced large-scale public uprisings and organized movements demanding integration into India, which significantly undermined the rulers' autonomy aspirations.
  • The Indian government also used plebiscites in certain cases, such as Junagadh, where the overwhelming public vote favored joining India, further showcasing popular sentiment as a decisive factor.

  • The consistent and unwavering support provided by the British government to the princely states seeking independence (Option 2):

    • The British policy of "paramountcy" ended, leaving princely rulers to decide between joining India, Pakistan, or remaining independent.
    • This option is incorrect because British influence was no longer a decisive factor post-1947.
  • The economic prosperity and stability offered by remaining independent, making integration into the financially strained Indian Union unattractive (Option 4):
    • While some rulers perceived independence as economically beneficial, the financial strain of maintaining sovereignty and administrative responsibilities outweighed perceived benefits.
    • Integration into India also provided economic stability, access to resources, and infrastructural development which were favorable compared to isolated independence.
    • Economic factors were secondary to popular will and political negotiations in determining accession.
  • Main Theme:
    • The integration of princely states into the Indian Union was a monumental task involving diplomacy, popular movements, and sometimes military intervention.
