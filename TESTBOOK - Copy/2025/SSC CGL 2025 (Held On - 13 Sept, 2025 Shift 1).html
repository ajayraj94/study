Question No. 21General Intelligence and Reasoning (Q.21)
A 60 L mixture has a milk-water ratio of 7:3. How much water to add to make the ratio 1:1?
A)
18 L
B)
20 L
C)
22 L
D)
24 L  ✓ (Correct Answer)
Correct Option: D) 24 L
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Initial Ratio (Milk : Water) = 7 : 3 (Total = 10 units)

Final Ratio = 1 : 1 → Equate Milk (since only water is added) → 7 : 7

Increase in Water units = 7 − 3 = 4 units

Given 10 units = 60 L ⇒ 1 unit = 6 L

Added Water = 4 units × 6 L = 24 L

∴ The correct answer is 24 L.

Alternate Method

Given: Total mixture = 60 L, Initial ratio (M:W) = 7 : 3, Final ratio (M:W) = 1 : 1.

⇒ Total parts in initial mixture = 7 + 3 = 10 units

⇒ Quantity of Milk = (7 ÷ 10) × 60 = 42 L

⇒ Quantity of Water = (3 ÷ 10) × 60 = 18 L

⇒ In the final 1 : 1 mixture, let the water added be x liters.

⇒ New Water content = 18 + x

⇒ Milk : Water = 42 : (18 + x) = 1 : 1

⇒ 42 = 18 + x

⇒ x = 42 − 18 = 24 L

∴ The correct answer is 24 L.

📌 Additional Information

Constant Quantity Concept

When one component of a mixture is added or removed, the quantity of the other component remains constant. Always equate the parts of the constant component in the ratio calculations.

Ratio Balancing

To simplify calculations, multiply the final ratio by a factor that makes the non-changing substance equal in both ratios (e.g., if milk is 7 parts initially, make it 7 parts in the final ratio).

Percentage Method

Initial Milk % = 70%. In 1:1 ratio, Milk % = 50%. Using inverse variation: Total Volume1 × %1 = Total Volume2 × %2 ⇒ 60 × 70 = Vnew × 50 ⇒ Vnew = 84 L.

📌 Key Points

  • The Bhakti movement in India emphasized devotion to a personal deity and was categorized into two main traditions: Saguna and Nirguna.
  • Saguna Bhakti refers to devotion to a deity with form and attributes, such as Lord Krishna, Rama, or Goddess Durga.
  • Nirguna Bhakti focuses on the worship of a formless, infinite, and abstract divine reality, often associated with concepts like the ultimate truth or Brahman.
  • Famous saints like Meera Bai, Tulsidas, and Surdas were proponents of Saguna Bhakti, while Kabir and Guru Nanak emphasized Nirguna Bhakti.
  • The two traditions reflect different approaches to spirituality, offering flexibility for individual beliefs and practices within the Bhakti movement.

📌 Additional Information

  • Vedic and Non-Vedic
    • The terms "Vedic" and "Non-Vedic" classify traditions based on their alignment with the Vedas.
    • Vedic traditions include rituals, sacrifices, and practices outlined in the Vedas, while Non-Vedic traditions involve independent or alternative spiritual practices, such as Jainism and Buddhism.
    • However, the Bhakti movement does not strictly align with this classification as it incorporates elements from both.
  • Orthodox and Reformist
    • The "Orthodox" approach adheres strictly to traditional practices and scriptures, while the "Reformist" approach focuses on modifying or reforming traditional religious practices.
    • While the Bhakti movement did involve some reformist ideas, it is not categorized within this binary classification.
  • Shaiva and Vaishnava
    • Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions refer to sects within Hinduism that worship Lord Shiva and Lord Vishnu, respectively, as the supreme deity.
    • These sects are part of the broader Bhakti traditions but are not the two main categories of Bhakti worship.

  • Examples of hard rocks where gorges are formed include granite, basalt, and other igneous or metamorphic rocks.
  • Famous gorges like the Grand Canyon in the USA and Kali Gandaki Gorge in Nepal were carved through hard rock formations by persistent river erosion.

    • Glacial till is an unsorted mixture of sediments deposited directly by glacier ice.
    • It does not form gorges, as it lacks the structural integrity required to create steep walls.

    • Volcanic ash consists of fine particles of volcanic glass and minerals ejected during volcanic eruptions.
