The Bimaran casket, discovered in Afghanistan, is a gold reliquary attributed to the Gandhara art style.   • Statement 2: Intricately carved ivory plaques were discovered at Begram—This is also correct. The Begram ivories, excavated from the ancient city of Begram in Afghanistan, are well-known examples of Gandhara art. These carvings reflect a blend of local and foreign artistic traditions, including Indian, Greco-Roman, and Central Asian influences.

  • Both statements collectively highlight the diversity of Gandhara art beyond stone sculptures, emphasizing its influence on various forms of artistic expression.

📌 Additional Information

  • Gandhara Art:
    • Gandhara art flourished during the Kushan Empire (1st–5th century CE) in the northwestern region of the Indian subcontinent, encompassing parts of modern-day Pakistan and Afghanistan.
    • It is known for its depiction of Buddhist themes, particularly the life of the Buddha, using techniques and styles influenced by Greco-Roman art.

    • This artifact demonstrates the influence of Hellenistic art on Buddhist iconography in the region.
  • Artifacts Found at Begram:
    • Begram, an ancient city on the Silk Road, yielded a treasure trove of artifacts, including intricately carved ivory plaques, glassware, and coins.
    • The Begram ivories, in particular, showcase exceptional craftsmanship and provide insights into the cultural exchanges between India, Central Asia, and the Mediterranean during ancient times.
