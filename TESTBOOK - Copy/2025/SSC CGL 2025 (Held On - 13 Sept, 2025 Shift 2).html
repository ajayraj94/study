Arrange the following sites in chronological order based on the stylistic development of Buddhist sculpture:

a) Mathura

b) Bharhut

b-c-a  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
c-a-b

c-b-a

a-c-b
Correct Option: A) b-c-a
Explanation:

The correct answer is b-c-a.

📌 Key Points

  • Bharhut (2nd century BCE): It represents the earliest phase of Buddhist art in India, with its relief sculptures being relatively simple and narrative in nature. The themes revolve around the Jataka tales and early Buddhist motifs.
  • Sanchi Stupa-1 (1st century BCE): Located in Madhya Pradesh, the Sanchi Stupa showcases more advanced artistic techniques, with intricate carvings, toranas (gateways), and a more evolved style of Buddhist representation.
  • Mathura (1st-2nd century CE): The Mathura school of art is known for creating Buddha and Bodhisattva sculptures in human form, transitioning from symbolic representation to anthropomorphic depictions.
  • The chronological sequence of the development of Buddhist sculptures starts with Bharhut, followed by Sanchi Stupa-1, and ends with Mathura, reflecting a clear evolution in artistic styles and techniques.
  • This progression demonstrates the shift from symbolic to anthropomorphic representation of Buddha in Indian art, marking the stylistic evolution of Buddhist sculpture over centuries.

📌 Additional Information

  • Bharhut:
    • Located in Madhya Pradesh, Bharhut is one of the earliest examples of Buddhist art and architecture.
    • The Bharhut stupa features narrative reliefs depicting Jataka tales and early Buddhist symbols like the Bodhi tree, footprints, and wheels.
    • The art here is relatively simple, with less emphasis on intricate detailing compared to later styles.
  • Sanchi Stupa-1:
    • The Great Stupa at Sanchi is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most significant monuments of Indian Buddhist architecture.
    • The intricately carved toranas (gateways) and railings depict scenes from the life of Buddha, Jataka tales, and Buddhist symbols.
    • The Sanchi sculptures show a transition toward more detailed and refined artistry compared to Bharhut.

    • The Mathura school of art developed during the Kushana period and is known for its anthropomorphic depictions of Buddha and Bodhisattvas.

    • Mathura art also influenced other Buddhist art forms, including the Gandhara school of art.

Question No. 28General Awareness (Q.3)
What is the term for a structured musical composition in Hindustani classical music?
A)
Kriti
B)
Bandish  ✓ (Correct Answer)
C)
Gamaka
D)
Rasa
Correct Option: B) Bandish
Explanation:

The correct answer is Bandish.

📌 Key Points

  • A Bandish is a fixed, melodic composition in Hindustani classical music, often set to a specific raga and taal.
  • It serves as the foundation for improvisation and is typically performed in a specific rhythmic cycle, such as Teentaal or Ektaal.
  • Bandish is composed of two main parts: the Sthayi (the initial part) and the Antara (the second part), which explore the raga's characteristics.
  • It is usually written in a specific language, such as Hindi, Urdu, Braj Bhasha, or Sanskrit, and often has devotional or romantic themes.
  • Bandishes are an integral part of the Gharana system, with each Gharana having its unique repertoire of compositions.

📌 Additional Information

  • Kriti
    • A Kriti is a structured composition in Carnatic classical music, not Hindustani music.
    • It consists of three main sections: Pallavi (opening), Anupallavi (middle section), and Charanam (ending).
    • Kritis often emphasize devotional themes and are composed in South Indian languages like Tamil, Telugu, or Kannada.
    • Famous Carnatic composers like Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri are known for their kritis.
  • Gamaka
    • Gamaka refers to the ornamentation or embellishment of notes in Indian classical music.
    • It involves techniques such as oscillation, slides, and bends to bring out the essence of a raga.
    • Gamaka is not a composition but a stylistic element used during performance.
    • It varies in execution between Hindustani and Carnatic music styles.
  • Rasa
    • Rasa refers to the aesthetic or emotional experience evoked in the listener by a musical performance.
    • Indian classical music often aims to evoke specific rasas, such as joy (Hasya), sorrow (Karuna), or peace (Shanta).
    • It is a broader concept rooted in Indian aesthetics and applies to various art forms, including dance and drama.

  • Frequency is the number of oscillations or cycles per second of a wave and is measured in Hertz (Hz).
  • According to the equation v = f × λ (where v is the speed, f is the frequency, and λ is the wavelength), frequency must remain unchanged during refraction to preserve energy conservation.

  • Speed:
    • Light travels at different speeds depending on the refractive index of the medium.
    • In air or vacuum, light travels at its maximum speed (approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s), but in denser media, the speed decreases.
    • The relationship between speed and refractive index is given by v = c / n, where c is the speed of light in a vacuum and n is the refractive index.

    • The amplitude of light represents the intensity or brightness of the wave.
    • While amplitude can change due to absorption or scattering in the medium, it is unrelated to the refractive properties of the medium.

  • Concept of Refraction:
    • Refraction occurs when light changes direction upon entering a medium with a different refractive index.
    • The refractive index is a measure of how much the medium slows down light compared to a vacuum.
