Question No. 19General Intelligence and Reasoning (Q.19)
A and B are partners in a business. A invests ₹20,000 for a period of 2 years. B invests ₹30,000 for a period of 3 years. If A&#39;s share of the total profit is ₹4,000, what is B&#39;s share of the profit?
A)
₹9,000  ✓ (Correct Answer)
B)
₹4,000
C)
₹5,000
D)
₹7,000
Correct Option: A) ₹9,000
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Profit ratio of A : B = (Capital × Time) ratio

A : B = (20,000 × 2) : (30,000 × 3) = 40,000 : 90,000 = 4 : 9

A's share = 4 units = ₹4,000

B's share = 9 units = (4,000 ÷ 4) × 9 = ₹9,000

∴ The correct answer is ₹9,000.

Alternate Method

Given:

Investment of A = ₹20,000, Time = 2 years

Investment of B = ₹30,000, Time = 3 years

Profit share of A = ₹4,000

Formula Used:

Profit Ratio = (Capital1 × Time1) : (Capital2 × Time2)

Calculations:

⇒ Effective capital of A = 20,000 × 2 = ₹40,000

⇒ Effective capital of B = 30,000 × 3 = ₹90,000

⇒ Ratio of profits (A : B) = 40,000 : 90,000 = 4 : 9

⇒ Let the common ratio factor be x.

⇒ A's share = 4x = 4,000

⇒ x = 4,000 ÷ 4 = 1,000

⇒ B's share = 9x = 9 × 1,000

⇒ B's share = ₹9,000

∴ The correct answer is ₹9,000.

📌 Additional Information

Basic Partnership Rule

Profit is always distributed in the ratio of the product of the capital invested and the time for which it is invested: Profit Ratio = C1T1 : C2T2.

Compound Partnership

When partners invest different amounts for different periods of time, it is called a compound partnership, requiring the use of weighted capital over time.

Types of Partners

An Active Partner manages the business and may take a salary before profit distribution, whereas a Sleeping Partner only invests money.

📌 Key Points

  • Kambala Festival is a traditional annual buffalo race held in the coastal regions of Karnataka, primarily in Udupi and Dakshina Kannada districts.
  • The festival involves pairs of buffaloes racing across muddy, slushy tracks while being controlled by their handlers, known as "jockeys."
  • Kambala is celebrated to honor the agricultural traditions and cultural heritage of the region, rooted in the worship of Bhoota (spirits) and nature.
  • Historically, Kambala was organized by farming communities as a form of thanksgiving to gods for protecting their crops and providing a good harvest.
  • Over time, the event transformed into a competitive sport, attracting participants and spectators from across Karnataka and neighboring states.

📌 Additional Information

  • Temple Fairs:
    • Temple fairs are religious events held in and around temples, particularly in South India, to celebrate local deities and cultural traditions.
    • These fairs often include rituals, processions, folk performances, stalls, and various community activities.
    • They play an important role in preserving local traditions and fostering community bonds.
    • Examples include the Mysuru Dasara festival and the Meenakshi Temple festival in Tamil Nadu.
  • Classical Music:
    • Karnataka is renowned for its contribution to Indian classical music, particularly Carnatic music, which originated in South India.
    • It focuses on intricate ragas (melodic frameworks) and talas (rhythmic cycles), emphasizing devotion and spirituality.
    • Prominent Carnatic musicians from Karnataka include Purandara Dasa (known as the father of Carnatic music) and Muthuswami Dikshitar.
    • Carnatic music is distinct from Hindustani classical music, which developed in North India.
  • Tribal Rituals:
    • Tribal rituals in India are traditional ceremonies practiced by indigenous communities across the country.
    • These rituals often involve nature worship, ancestor worship, and celebrations of tribal folklore and legends.
    • Such practices are integral to preserving the unique cultural identity of tribal groups.
    • In Karnataka, tribes like Soliga, Koraga, and Jenukuruba have their own distinct rituals and traditions.
  • Main Theme – Kambala Festival:
    • Kambala is deeply rooted in the agrarian culture of Karnataka, reflecting the harmonious relationship between humans, animals, and nature.
    • While primarily celebrated as a sporting event today, it continues to serve as a reminder of the agricultural heritage and spiritual beliefs of the region.
