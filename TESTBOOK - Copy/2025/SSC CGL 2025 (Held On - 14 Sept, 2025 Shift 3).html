Question No. 22General Intelligence and Reasoning (Q.22)
A sum becomes 2.5 times in 10 years under simple interest. Find the rate.
A)
10%
B)
12.5%
C)
15%  ✓ (Correct Answer)
D)
20%
Correct Option: C) 15%
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Rate (R) = [(n − 1) × 100] ÷ Time

Here, n = 2.5 and Time = 10 years.

R = [(2.5 − 1) × 100] ÷ 10 = [1.5 × 100] ÷ 10 = 150 ÷ 10 = 15%.

∴ The correct answer is 15%.

Alternate Method

Given: Principal (P) = P, Amount (A) = 2.5P, Time (T) = 10 years.

Formula Used: Simple Interest (SI) = (P × R × T) ÷ 100; SI = Amount − Principal

⇒ SI = 2.5P − P = 1.5P

⇒ 1.5P = (P × R × 10) ÷ 100

⇒ 1.5 = (R × 10) ÷ 100

⇒ 1.5 = R ÷ 10

⇒ R = 1.5 × 10 = 15%

∴ The correct answer is 15%.

📌 Additional Information

Simple Interest Formula

SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100, where P is Principal, R is Rate per annum, and T is Time in years.

Sum Multiplier Rule

If a sum becomes 'n' times in 'T' years, the rate R = [(n − 1) ÷ T] × 100.

Amount in Simple Interest

Amount (A) = P + SI = P[1 + (RT ÷ 100)].

Statement II is true but statement I is false
Correct Option: C) Statement I is true but Statement II is false
Explanation:

The correct answer is Statement I is true but Statement II is false.

📌 Key Points

  • The harmika of a stupa is a small, square railing located at the top of the dome (anda) and is considered the symbolic abode of the gods.
  • It is an important architectural feature of stupas and signifies the connection between the earthly realm and the divine.
  • The harmika is typically surmounted by a mast (yasti) with umbrellas or chhatras, symbolizing honor and protection.
  • Statement II is incorrect because the relics of the Buddha or other revered figures are not kept in the harmika; they are enshrined in the anda (the hemispherical dome) of the stupa.
  • The anda serves as the primary burial mound or relic chamber, which houses sacred relics and is the focal point of veneration.

📌 Additional Information

  • Components of a Stupa:
    • Anda (Dome): The hemispherical dome represents the mound of dirt used to cover the Buddha's relics. It is the most important part of the stupa, symbolizing the universe.
    • Harmika: A square platform at the top of the dome, representing the abode of gods. It does not contain relics but serves as a symbolic feature.
    • Chhatra: The umbrella-like structure above the harmika symbolizes protection and honor. It often represents the Three Jewels of Buddhism: the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.
    • Medhi: A circular terrace at the base of the dome, used by devotees for circumambulation (pradakshina).
    • Torana: The gateway of the stupa, typically elaborately carved, representing the entry point for devotees.

  • It is divided into two parts: the Shukla (White) Yajurveda, which contains clear and organized rituals, and the Krishna (Black) Yajurveda, which includes a mix of mantras and explanatory prose.
  • The Yajurveda is written in Sanskrit and focuses on the performance of Vedic sacrificial rituals to invoke deities and ensure harmony in the universe.
  • Its primary purpose is to provide a detailed framework for conducting ceremonial offerings, including fire sacrifices, to maintain cosmic order and promote spiritual well-being.

📌 Additional Information

    • The hymns in the Rigveda were recited during rituals but are less focused on procedural details compared to the Yajurveda.

    • The Samaveda is known as the "Veda of Melodies" and primarily consists of hymns from the Rigveda set to music.
    • It is meant to be sung during Vedic sacrifices and rituals, especially during the Soma sacrifice.
    • The Samaveda highlights the importance of sound and music in invoking divine forces and creating a sacred atmosphere.
  • Atharvaveda
    • The Atharvaveda contains hymns, spells, and incantations that deal with everyday life, health, and healing, as well as spiritual practices.
    • It includes knowledge of Ayurveda, focusing on remedies for diseases and methods to ward off negative influences.
    • Unlike the other three Vedas, it is more practical and addresses the needs of common people.

  • The cooling of the rising air leads to the condensation of water vapor present in it, resulting in the formation of clouds.
  • Once the clouds become saturated, they release moisture in the form of rainfall, which is termed convectional rainfall.
  • This type of rainfall is most common in regions near the equator, such as tropical areas, where the sun's heat is intense year-round.

📌 Additional Information

  • Cold fronts meeting warm air
    • This phenomenon typically leads to frontal rainfall, not convectional rainfall.

    • This type of rainfall is common in temperate regions and is often associated with cyclonic weather systems.
  • Mountains forcing air to rise
    • This process results in orographic or relief rainfall, not convectional rainfall.
    • When moist air is forced to ascend over mountain ranges, it cools and condenses, leading to rainfall on the windward side of the mountains.
    • The leeward side, also known as the rain shadow region, typically experiences dry conditions.
  • Moist air from polar regions
    • Moist air from polar regions is not associated with convectional rainfall.
    • Instead, it plays a role in polar climates, where precipitation is typically in the form of snow rather than rain.
    • Polar regions experience low precipitation levels due to the cold temperatures and limited moisture in the air.
