Question No. 23General Intelligence and Reasoning (Q.23)
Two numbers are in ratio 7:9, and their sum is 128. Find the numbers.
A)
49, 79
B)
56, 72  ✓ (Correct Answer)
C)
62, 66
D)
70, 58
Correct Option: B) 56, 72
Explanation:
📌 Shortcut Trick

Total units in the ratio = 7 + 9 = 16 units.

Given Sum = 128, so 16 units = 128 → 1 unit = 128 ÷ 16 = 8.

First number = 7 × 8 = 56.

Second number = 9 × 8 = 72.

∴ The correct answer is 56, 72.

Alternate Method

Given: Ratio of two numbers = 7 : 9 and Sum of numbers = 128.

Formula Used: If a sum S is divided in the ratio a : b, then the parts are (a / (a + b)) × S and (b / (a + b)) × S.

⇒ Let the two numbers be 7x and 9x.

⇒ According to the question, 7x + 9x = 128

⇒ 16x = 128

⇒ x = 128 ÷ 16

⇒ x = 8

⇒ First Number = 7 × 8 = 56

⇒ Second Number = 9 × 8 = 72

∴ The correct answer is 56, 72.

📌 Additional Information

Ratio Definition

A ratio is a comparison of two or more quantities of the same kind by division. For a : b, 'a' is called the antecedent and 'b' is the consequent.

Inverse Ratio

The inverse ratio of a : b is b : a. This is commonly used in speed-time and work-efficiency problems.

Compounded Ratio

For two ratios a : b and c : d, the compounded ratio is (a × c) : (b × d).

Proportion

When two ratios are equal, they are said to be in proportion (a : b :: c : d), implying the product of extremes equals the product of means (ad = bc).

Which of the following correctly reflects the evolution of Indian classical music?

Jatis evolved from ragas as per ancient texts

Explanation:

The correct answer is Ragas evolved from jatis; 72-mela was by Venkatamakhi.

📌 Key Points

  • Ragas evolved from jatis: The concept of jatis (a classification of musical scales) formed the basis of Indian classical music, and over time, these evolved into the more structured and expressive ragas. This evolution can be traced back to ancient Indian music treatises such as the Natya Shastra, which describes jatis in detail. Hence, this statement is correct.
  • 72-mela system by Venkatamakhi: The 72-mela system (or Melakarta system) was developed in the 17th century by the South Indian musicologist Venkatamakhi. It is a systematic arrangement of 72 parent ragas (melas) that form the foundation of Carnatic music. Hence, this statement is correct.
  • Jatis evolved from ragas: This statement is incorrect because historical evidence and ancient texts confirm that jatis preceded ragas. Jatis were simpler and less structured, while ragas represent a more developed form of musical expression.
  • Bharata and the 72-mela system: The statement that Bharata developed the 72-mela system is incorrect. Bharata, the author of the Natya Shastra, lived around the 2nd century BCE and is credited with describing the earliest concepts of music, drama, and dance. The 72-mela system was introduced much later by Venkatamakhi.
  • Folk music and mela/grama scales: Folk music did not originally use the structured mela and grama scales. These are theoretical constructs of classical music and are not traditionally associated with folk music. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

📌 Additional Information

  • Jatis:
    • Jatis were the precursors to ragas and are mentioned extensively in the Natya Shastra by Bharata.
    • They described specific tonal sequences and were categorized into shuddha (pure), bhinna (divergent), and gaudi (regional) types.
    • The development from jatis to ragas marked the transition from ancient to medieval Indian music.
  • Ragas:
    • A raga is a melodic framework for improvisation and composition in Indian classical music.
    • Ragas are structured around specific notes (swaras) and rules for their progression, emphasizing aesthetics and emotional expression.
    • Each raga is associated with a particular time of day, season, or mood (rasa).
  • 72-Melakarta System:
    • The Melakarta system categorizes parent ragas into 72 groups based on their scale structure (ascending and descending note patterns).
    • It is fundamental to Carnatic music and aids in the systematic study and composition of ragas.
    • Venkatamakhi's work, Chaturdandi Prakasika, outlines this system in detail.
  • Role of Bharata:
    • Bharata is regarded as the father of Indian classical music and dance due to his seminal work, the Natya Shastra.
    • He provided the first theoretical framework for Indian music, including swaras, gramas (scales), and talas (rhythmic patterns).
  • Folk Music vs Classical Music:
    • Folk music is informal and often specific to regions, communities, or traditions.
    • It lacks the rigid theoretical frameworks of classical music, which are based on ragas, talas, and structured improvisations.

📌 Important Points

  • Natya Shastra: Written by Bharata, it is one of the earliest treatises on performing arts in India, covering music, drama, and dance.
  • Sangeet Ratnakara: A 13th-century text by Sharangadeva that bridges the gap between ancient and medieval Indian music traditions.
  • Rasa Theory: A key concept in Indian aesthetics, linking music and art with emotional expression.

  • Slumps occur when a slope becomes unstable due to water infiltration, weak soil, or geological disturbances.
  • They are commonly found in areas with clay-rich soils, as clay can become saturated and lose its cohesive strength.
  • Unlike other types of landslides, slumps usually involve large blocks of material moving as a coherent unit, rather than chaotic debris flow.

📌 Additional Information

  • Rockslide:
    • A rockslide involves the rapid movement of large masses of bedrock downslope.
    • It typically occurs on steep slopes where rock layers are weakened by fractures or weathering.
    • Unlike slumps, rockslides do not rotate; instead, they slide along a planar surface.
    • Example: The Frank Slide in Canada (1903).
  • Rock fall:
    • A rock fall is characterized by the free fall or bouncing of individual rocks from steep cliffs or slopes.
    • It is often triggered by freeze-thaw cycles, erosion, or seismic activity.
    • Rock falls are the fastest type of landslide and can cause significant damage.
    • Example: Yosemite National Park has frequent rock falls due to its granite cliffs.
  • Debris fall:
    • Debris falls involve the rapid downward movement of unconsolidated material, such as soil, rocks, and organic matter.
    • They are often triggered by heavy rainfall or earthquakes, which destabilize the slope.
    • Debris falls are chaotic and lack the coherent rotational movement seen in slumps.
    • Example: The 2014 Oso landslide in Washington, USA, was a debris fall.
  • Characteristics of Slumps:
    • Slumps are slower than rockslides and debris falls due to their rotational movement.
    • They are common in areas with poorly consolidated sediments, especially in regions prone to water saturation.
