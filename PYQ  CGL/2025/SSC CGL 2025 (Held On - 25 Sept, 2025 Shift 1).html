Explanation:

The correct answer is Humayun's Tomb.

📌 Key Points

  • Humayun's Tomb, located in Delhi, India, served as a major inspiration for the design and architecture of the Taj Mahal.
  • It was built in 1565-1572 by Empress Bega Begum, the wife of Mughal Emperor Humayun, and is considered the first garden-tomb in the Indian subcontinent.
  • Humayun's Tomb is known for its distinctive Persian-style architecture, which features a symmetrical layout, a double dome, and intricate carvings.
  • The architectural elements of Humayun's Tomb, including the use of white marble and red sandstone, influenced the construction of the Taj Mahal in Agra.
  • The Taj Mahal, built by Emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal, incorporated the aesthetic principles seen in Humayun's Tomb, particularly the symmetry and Mughal garden layout.

📌 Additional Information

  • Humayun's Tomb:
    • Located in Delhi, India, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
    • It was the first structure in India to use red sandstone on a large scale.
    • The tomb was designed by Mirak Mirza Ghiyas, a Persian architect.
    • Its design features a charbagh (four-part garden), a hallmark of Mughal architecture.
  • Taj Mahal:
    • Built in 1632-1653 in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal.
    • It is made of white marble and is recognized as one of the Seven Wonders of the World.
    • The layout of the Taj Mahal is inspired by the Persian-style charbagh gardens seen in Humayun's Tomb.
    • The Taj Mahal is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site and symbolizes the zenith of Mughal architecture.
  • Other Options Explained:
    • Jama Masjid: Built by Shah Jahan in Delhi, it is one of the largest mosques in India but does not share direct architectural influence with the Taj Mahal.
    • Victoria Memorial: Located in Kolkata, this structure was built during British rule and has no connection to Mughal architecture or the Taj Mahal.
    • Qutub Minar: A UNESCO World Heritage Site in Delhi, it is an ancient minaret from the Delhi Sultanate period and is unrelated to the architectural inspiration of the Taj Mahal.
  • 📌 Important Points
    • The use of symmetry, domes, and gardens in Mughal architecture is a defining characteristic that connects Humayun's Tomb and the Taj Mahal.
    • Both structures reflect the fusion of Persian, Islamic, and Indian architectural styles.

Question No. 28General Awareness (Q.3)
Which act introduced the system of dyarchy (dual government) in the provinces of British India?
A)
The Indian Councils Act of 1861
B)
The Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909
C)
The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919  ✓ (Correct Answer)
D)
The Government of India Act of 1935
Correct Option: C) The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919
Explanation:

The correct answer is The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919.

📌 Key Points

  • The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919 introduced the system of dyarchy (dual government) in the provinces of British India. This was a major constitutional reform aimed at increasing Indian participation in governance.
  • Under dyarchy, the provincial subjects were divided into two categories: Transferred and Reserved.
    • Transferred subjects: These included areas like education, public health, agriculture, and local government, which were administered by Indian ministers responsible to the legislative councils.
    • Reserved subjects: These included areas like finance, police, and revenue, which were controlled by the British Governor and his executive council.
  • The reforms were based on the Montagu-Chelmsford Report of 1918, authored by Edwin Montagu (Secretary of State for India) and Lord Chelmsford (Viceroy of India).
  • The objective of the reforms was to gradually introduce a responsible government in India while retaining British control over vital areas of administration.
  • The system of dyarchy, however, was heavily criticized due to its complexity and inefficiency. It failed to meet the aspirations of Indian leaders and was eventually replaced by provincial autonomy under the Government of India Act of 1935.

📌 Additional Information

  • The Indian Councils Act of 1861:
    • This Act marked the beginning of legislative devolution by allowing Indians to be included in the law-making process as non-official members in the Viceroy's council.
    • It laid the foundation for representative institutions in India but did not introduce dyarchy.
  • The Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909:
    • Also known as the Indian Councils Act of 1909, this reform introduced the concept of separate electorates for Muslims.
    • It allowed for greater Indian representation in legislative councils but did not involve the division of subjects or dyarchy.
  • The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919:
    • Introduced dyarchy in provinces, which was a significant shift towards self-governance.
    • It also expanded the legislative councils and introduced direct elections for some seats.
    • This reform emphasized the principle of increasing Indian participation in governance.
  • The Government of India Act of 1935:
    • This Act replaced dyarchy with provincial autonomy, granting full control of provincial administration to Indian ministers.
    • It also proposed the establishment of a federation of British India and princely states, though this was never implemented.

📌 Important Points

  • Dyarchy:
    • It was the first step towards decentralization of power in British India.
    • The system was criticized because the reserved subjects, controlled by the British, were often the more critical and resource-intensive areas.
  • Failures of the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms:
    • The division of subjects created confusion and inefficiency in governance.
    • Indian leaders criticized the reforms for not providing full self-governance or addressing key issues like taxation and economic exploitation.
  • Historical Context: The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms were introduced in the aftermath of World War I when India contributed significantly to the war effort. The reforms were a response to increasing demands for political reforms and self-governance.
  • Legacy: Though the dyarchy system was short-lived, it laid the groundwork for future reforms, eventually leading to India's independence in 1947.

  • Object at the center of curvature (C): When an object is placed at the center of curvature of a concave mirror, the image is formed at the same location as the object, i.e., at C.
  • Nature of the image: The image formed is real, inverted, and of the same size as the object.
  • Reflection mechanics: In this position, light rays originating from the object strike the mirror and converge at the same distance, creating an image identical in size and orientation (except inverted).
  • Ray diagram: A ray diagram of a concave mirror with the object at C clearly shows the reflected rays intersecting at the same point on the other side of the mirror, confirming the image characteristics.

📌 Additional Information

  • Concave Mirror Basics:
    • A concave mirror is a spherical mirror with its reflective surface curving inward, resembling the interior of a sphere.
    • It is also known as a converging mirror because it converges light rays that fall on its surface.
  • Image Characteristics by Object Position:
    • Beyond C: Image is real, inverted, and diminished.
    • At C: Image is real, inverted, and of the same size.
    • Between C and F: Image is real, inverted, and magnified.
    • At F: Image is highly magnified and formed at infinity.
    • Between F and Mirror: Image is virtual, erect, and magnified.
  • Applications of Concave Mirrors:
    • Shaving mirrors: Provide a magnified and upright image.
    • Telescope: Used in reflecting telescopes to gather and focus light.
    • Headlights: Used in car headlights to focus light into a beam.
  • Real vs. Virtual Images:
    • Real images: Formed when light rays converge and can be projected on a screen.
    • Virtual images: Formed when light rays only appear to diverge and cannot be projected on a screen.

📌 Important Points

    • f: Focal length of the mirror.
    • v: Image distance from the mirror.
    • u: Object distance from the mirror.
  • Magnification: The magnification (M) of a mirror is given by M = -v/u. For an object at C, magnification is -1 (image is of the same size but inverted).
  • Principal Focus and Radius of Curvature:
    • The distance between the mirror and the principal focus is called the focal length (f).
    • The radius of curvature (R) is twice the focal length (R = 2f).

  • Behavior of Consumers: Higher prices typically deter consumers from purchasing more of a good or service, while lower prices encourage increased consumption, assuming no other factors change.
  • Demand Curve Representation: Graphically, the law of demand is represented by a downward-sloping demand curve, where price is plotted on the vertical axis and quantity demanded on the horizontal axis.
  • Incorrect Statements: Other options such as "As price increases, demand increases" or "Price has no effect on demand" are inconsistent with the law of demand and do not hold true under normal market conditions.
