📌 Key Points

  • Hitting the ball twice deliberately is considered an illegal action in cricket and results in dismissal under the laws of the game.
  • This dismissal is covered under Law 34 of the MCC Laws of Cricket, which prohibits a batter from intentionally striking the ball twice unless it is done to defend their wicket.
  • Such an action is judged by the umpire, who determines whether the batter deliberately hit the ball twice.
  • The first strike is considered legitimate (e.g., hitting the ball with the bat), but the second strike intended to gain runs or mislead the fielding team constitutes a breach.
  • This rule upholds the integrity of fair play in cricket and ensures that the batter does not use unfair methods to gain an advantage.

📌 Additional Information

  • Retired Out:
    • A player retiring out does not need umpire approval; it is the batter's voluntary decision.
    • Unlike "retired hurt," where a player leaves due to injury with an option to return, "retired out" is final and does not allow re-entry into the game.
    • This form of dismissal is uncommon and usually occurs in practice matches or under special circumstances.
  • Obstructing the Field:
    • According to Law 37 of the MCC Laws of Cricket, "Obstructing the Field" occurs when a batter deliberately interferes with a fielder attempting to field or catch the ball.
    • This dismissal is not accidental; it requires clear intent by the batter to obstruct the fielding side.
    • Examples include using the bat or body to stop the ball while a fielder is attempting to effect a run-out or catch.
    • Accidental interference, such as running in the path of a fielder without intent, does not constitute this dismissal.
  • Cricket Dismissals:
    • There are several ways a batter can be dismissed in cricket, including bowled, caught, leg before wicket (LBW), stumped, and run-out.
    • Other less common dismissals include obstructing the field, retired out, hitting the ball twice, and timed out.
