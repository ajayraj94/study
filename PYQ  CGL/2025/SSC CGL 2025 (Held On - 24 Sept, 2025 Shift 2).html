Which of the following is a key requirement for admissibility of an electronic record under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), even if it's a secondary evidence?
A)
It must be handwritten and notarized
B)
It must be part of a hard drive submitted to court
C)
It must be published in the Gazette
D)
It must be accompanied by a certificate  ✓ (Correct Answer)
Correct Option: D) It must be accompanied by a certificate
Explanation:

The correct answer is It must be accompanied by a certificate.

📌 Key Points

  • Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (Indian Evidence Act), 1872, electronic records can be considered admissible as evidence only if they meet certain conditions.
  • The certificate requirement is specified under Section 65B(4) of the Act, which mandates that a certificate must accompany any electronic record submitted as secondary evidence.
  • This certificate must include details about the electronic device used to produce the record, confirming its authenticity and the method by which the data was generated.
  • The certificate is typically issued by a person in charge of the device (like a system administrator) and must be signed, providing assurance that the electronic record has not been tampered with.
  • Without this certificate, the electronic record cannot be admitted as secondary evidence, even if it is relevant to the case.

📌 Additional Information

  • Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act:
    • This section deals with the admissibility of electronic records as evidence in legal proceedings.
    • It specifies that for electronic records to be admissible, they must meet the conditions outlined in the section, including the requirement of a certificate under Sub-section 4.
    • This provision ensures that the integrity and reliability of electronic evidence are maintained.
  • Purpose of the Certificate:
    • The certificate serves as a safeguard against forgery, manipulation, or alteration of electronic records.
    • It confirms that the information in the electronic record was produced by a reliable device and process.
    • It provides details about the person responsible for the operation of the device and the accuracy of the record.
  • Other Relevant Provisions:
    • Section 65A: Introduces the concept of electronic records as evidence under specific circumstances.
    • Section 3: Defines the term "evidence" to include all documents, including electronic records, produced for the inspection of the court.
    • Section 67A: Discusses the proof of digital signatures and their verification.
  • Examples of Admissible Electronic Records:
    • Emails, text messages, and chat logs accompanied by a valid Section 65B certificate.
    • Printed copies of financial records generated from computer systems with an authenticated certificate.

📌 Important Points

  • Relevance of Electronic Records: With the rise of digital transactions, electronic records have become crucial in legal cases involving contracts, financial transactions, and communications.
  • Judicial Precedent: In the landmark judgment Anvar P.V. vs. P.K. Basheer (2014), the Supreme Court of India ruled that electronic records without a Section 65B certificate are inadmissible as evidence.
  • Global Practices: Many countries, including the United States and United Kingdom, have similar provisions for the admissibility of electronic evidence to ensure its reliability and authenticity.
  • Exceptions: If the electronic record is presented as primary evidence (e.g., the original hard drive or device), the certificate requirement may not apply.

Use of sandstone and floral arches

Hellenistic drapery and realism

D)
Predominantly wooden monastery design
Correct Option: C) Indigenous features with Yaksha influence
Explanation:

The correct answer is Indigenous features with Yaksha influence.

📌 Key Points

  • The Mathura School of Buddhist Art, which thrived during the Kushan period (1st century CE to 3rd century CE), is characterized by indigenous artistic traditions with strong influences of Yaksha and Yakshi iconography.
  • Unlike the Gandhara School, which exhibited Hellenistic features, the Mathura School evolved from local traditions and prominently displayed Indian motifs, techniques, and cultural themes.
  • The Yaksha influence is evident in the robust and cheerful figures of Buddha and Bodhisattvas, which were depicted as wearing simple drapery and adorned with ornaments.
  • Mathura sculptures were primarily made of red sandstone, which was locally available and had a smooth finish. This material contributed to the unique aesthetic of the Mathura art style.
  • Mathura artists developed the concept of anthropomorphic representations of Buddha, marking a significant departure from earlier symbolic depictions like stupas or footprints.

📌 Additional Information

  • Comparison with Gandhara School:
    • The Gandhara School, which developed in present-day Pakistan and Afghanistan, is known for its Greco-Roman influence. It used grey schist stone and displayed features like curly hair, realistic anatomy, and flowing drapery.
    • In contrast, the Mathura School focused on Indian traditions and emphasized cheerful expressions and indigenous themes.
  • Yaksha Influence in Indian Art:
    • Yakshas and Yakshis are ancient nature spirits associated with fertility, abundance, and protection. Their statues often depicted rounded, corpulent figures with lively expressions.
    • The Mathura School adopted these stylistic features to create Buddha and Bodhisattva figures that emphasized spiritual vitality.
  • Significance of Red Sandstone:
    • Red sandstone was widely used by Mathura artists due to its local availability. The smooth and durable nature of the stone allowed intricate carvings and polished finishes.
    • This material distinguished Mathura sculptures from those of the Gandhara School, which predominantly used grey schist stone.
  • Evolution of Anthropomorphic Buddha:
    • The Mathura School played a crucial role in transitioning from symbolic representations of Buddha (like stupas, wheels, or footprints) to anthropomorphic depictions.
    • Buddha statues from Mathura often depicted him in a seated or standing posture, with a serene expression and a halo behind his head.
  • Cultural Impact:
    • The Mathura School influenced later Indian art forms, including the Gupta period, which is considered the Golden Age of Indian art and architecture.
    • Its emphasis on indigenous themes and techniques contributed to the uniquely Indian identity of Buddhist art.

📌 Important Points

  • Historical Context:
    • The Mathura School flourished under Kushan rulers, who were patrons of art and architecture. Their support facilitated the development of Buddhist art in Mathura.
    • Mathura was an important center for religious and cultural activities during this period, contributing to its prominence in artistic innovations.
  • Examples of Mathura Art:
    • Iconic Buddha sculptures with distinct features like a halo, urna (mark on the forehead), and elongated ears.
    • Yaksha statues and images of Hindu deities, which showcased the versatility of Mathura artists.
  • Legacy:
    • The artistic traditions of the Mathura School influenced subsequent developments in Indian art, especially during the Gupta period.
    • Its emphasis on indigenous elements established a foundation for the evolution of Indian artistic identity.

📌 Key Points

  • Spherical mirrors are curved mirrors with a spherical surface, either concave or convex, used to reflect light.
  • To locate the image formed by a spherical mirror, at least two rays are required to determine the position and characteristics of the image.
  • The first ray is typically drawn parallel to the principal axis and reflects through the focus of the mirror.
  • The second ray is drawn passing through the focus (in case of concave mirrors) or appearing to pass through the focus (in case of convex mirrors) and reflects parallel to the principal axis.
  • These two reflected rays intersect (or appear to intersect) at a point, which provides the location of the image.

📌 Additional Information

  • Types of spherical mirrors:
    • Concave mirrors: These mirrors have an inward curved surface and are used to focus light. They can form real and virtual images depending on the position of the object.
    • Convex mirrors: These mirrors have an outward curved surface and always form virtual, upright, and diminished images.
  • Principal axis: It is an imaginary line passing through the center of curvature and the pole of the spherical mirror.
  • Focus: The point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the principal axis converge (concave mirror) or appear to diverge from (convex mirror) after reflection.
  • Image formation rules:
    • Ray parallel to the principal axis reflects through the focus.
    • Ray passing through the focus reflects parallel to the principal axis.
    • Ray passing through the center of curvature reflects back along the same path.
  • Applications: Spherical mirrors are used in telescopes, headlights, shaving mirrors, and rear-view mirrors in vehicles.

📌 Important Points

  • Ray diagrams: Ray diagrams are essential tools for understanding image formation by spherical mirrors. They use two or more rays to determine the image's position, size, orientation, and type.
  • Real vs. Virtual images:
    • Real images: Formed when reflected rays converge and can be projected on a screen.
    • Virtual images: Formed when reflected rays appear to diverge from a point and cannot be projected on a screen.
  • Mirror formula: The relationship between object distance (u), image distance (v), and focal length (f) is given by 1/f = 1/v + 1/u.
  • Magnification: Magnification (M) is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object and is given by M = -v/u.
  • Center of curvature: The center of the spherical surface of which the mirror forms a part. It is denoted by C.

The tangent to a circle, the radius, and the distance from the point to the center form a right-angled triangle.

Alternate Method

Given:

Radius of the circle (OT) = 5 cm

Distance of point P from the center O (OP) = 13 cm

Formula Used:

Pythagoras Theorem: (Hypotenuse)2 = (Perpendicular)2 + (Base)2

Calculations:

⇒ Let PT be the tangent to the circle at point T.

⇒ A key property of circles is that the radius drawn to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent line.

⇒ ∴ ∠OTP = 90°

⇒ In right-angled triangle OTP, applying Pythagoras theorem:

⇒ (OP)2 = (OT)2 + (PT)2

⇒ (13)2 = (5)2 + (PT)2

⇒ 169 = 25 + (PT)2

⇒ (PT)2 = 144

Tangent to a Circle

A tangent is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one distinct point. The radius intersecting this point of contact is always perpendicular to the tangent line.

Direct Common Tangent (DCT)

The length of a TCT between two circles with radii r1 and r2 and a central distance d is given by the formula √[d2 − (r1 + r2)2].
