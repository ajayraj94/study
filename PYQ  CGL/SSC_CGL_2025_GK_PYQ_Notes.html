Parliament & legislation

  • Money Bill: only Lok Sabha; Speaker certifies; Rajya Sabha can only recommend within 14 days.
  • Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill; every Financial Bill is not a Money Bill.
  • Guillotine: demands for grants put to vote after the time limit for discussion.
  • Rajya Sabha is a continuing house; members serve 6 years; one-third retire every 2 years.
  • Rajya Sabha special powers: Art 249 (State List) and Art 312 (new All India Service).
  • State Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Assembly — Art 164.
  • Governor can dissolve the Assembly; the President dissolves the Lok Sabha on the PM's advice.

Executive & emergency

  • President: minimum age 35; elected by electoral college; Governor is appointed by the President.
  • Governor may reserve a State Bill for the President; President's Rule under Art 356.
  • Financial Emergency: salaries of judges can be reduced by the President.
  • Art 370 abrogated through a Presidential Order under 370(3).

Judiciary, commissions & amendments

  • Doctrine of Severability: only the valid part of a law is retained.
  • SC: original jurisdiction Art 131; transfer of cases Art 139A.
  • Finance Commission (Art 280) — advisory; CAG audits; Election Commission conducts elections.
  • 42nd — “Mini-Constitution”; education moved to Concurrent List; added 139A.
  • 44th — removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights.
  • 73rd — Panchayati Raj institutions as units of local self-governance.
  • 86th — Art 21A: free and compulsory education, age 6–14.
  • 101st — GST; Art 246A, Art 279A; GST Council constitutional.
  • 104th — abolished Anglo-Indian representation in legislatures.
  • GST Council: Centre has 1/3 weight, States collectively 2/3; decisions by 3/4 of weighted votes present and voting.
  • Art 246A: both Parliament and States can legislate on GST; States tax intra-State supply.

Criminal law — the new trio

New lawReplaced / key point
BNS 2023Replaced IPC 1860; substantive criminal law; defines organized crime and petty organized crime; cyber identity theft.
BNSS 2023Replaced CrPC 1973; police custody max 15 days; default bail 60 days (<10 yrs) / 90 days; Zero FIR at any police station; e-FIR, e-summons, e-warrants; Sec 105 video-recorded search/seizure; video conferencing for vulnerable witnesses.
BSA 2023Replaced Indian Evidence Act 1872; electronic records as primary evidence; certificate needed for admissibility; digital signature; expert witnesses; oral evidence must be direct.

Rights, DPSP & UCC

  • Fundamental Rights are justiciable; DPSPs are not.
  • Art 14 — reasonable classification; landmark: State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar.
  • UCC is a DPSP (Art 44); Uttarakhand is the first State to implement it; Goa Civil Code applies to all communities.
  • Art 50: judiciary separated from executive — a DPSP.

National income & budget — formulas

  • GDP excludes the value of intermediate goods to avoid double counting.
  • GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad; NNP = GNP − depreciation.
  • Real GDP uses constant/base-year prices; nominal GDP uses current prices.
  • Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts. It signals borrowing for current spending.
  • Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − total receipts excluding borrowings.
  • Capital receipts create a liability or reduce assets — e.g. loan from RBI, disinvestment.
  • Revenue receipts create no repayment burden — taxes, dividends from PSUs.
  • Disinvestment = selling government stake in PSUs; minority sale transfers partial ownership.
  • Navratnas = public enterprises granted autonomy for better performance.

1991 reforms — LPG

  • 1991 crisis: foreign-exchange shortage; approached the IMF and World Bank.
  • LPG = Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation; “P” = Privatisation.
  • Industrial licensing abolished for most industries; retained for alcohol, cigarettes, explosives.
  • Washington Consensus = liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation; not export subsidies.
  • WTO — fair trade rules; globalisation allowed MNCs into India.
  • Import substitution — protect infant domestic industries; replace imports with domestic production.

Money, banking & inflation

  • Repo rate — rate at which RBI lends to banks; reverse repo — RBI borrows from banks.
  • CRR — cash reserve ratio; SLR — statutory liquidity ratio. Higher CRR/SLR reduce lending capacity.
  • Money multiplier — cash held by the public is a leakage and reduces the multiplier; higher bank reserves also reduce it.
  • Lead Bank Scheme — introduced in 1969.
  • Modern currencies are not readily convertible into gold.
  • CPI — retail inflation; WPI — wholesale inflation; core inflation excludes food and fuel.
  • 2024 decline in core inflation — mainly falling international commodity prices.
