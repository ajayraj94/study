Question No. 26General Awareness (Q.1)
What distinguishes ISRO's Aditya-L1 mission from other solar observatories?
A)
It uses radar waves to study the Sun
B)
It orbits the Sun directly
C)
It will study the solar corona from Lagrange Point 1  ✓ (Correct Answer)
D)
It is placed in a lunar orbit
Correct Option: C) It will study the solar corona from Lagrange Point 1
Explanation:

The correct answer is It will study the solar corona from Lagrange Point 1.

📌 Key Points

  • Aditya-L1 Mission: The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) launched the Aditya-L1 mission as India's first dedicated solar observatory. It is designed to study the Sun's atmosphere, including its corona, chromosphere, and photosphere.
  • Lagrange Point 1 (L1): The spacecraft is positioned at Lagrange Point 1, which is located approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth. This unique location provides an uninterrupted view of the Sun without being affected by Earth's shadow.
  • Study of Solar Corona: The Aditya-L1 mission focuses on studying the solar corona, the outermost layer of the Sun. The corona plays a critical role in understanding solar activities such as solar flares and coronal mass ejections, which can impact Earth's climate and satellite communications.
  • Scientific Instruments: The mission carries a suite of seven payloads designed to observe various layers of the Sun and analyze its magnetic field, solar wind, and particle behavior. These instruments aim to provide comprehensive data for solar physics research.
  • Significance of L1 Point: Lagrange Point 1 is strategically chosen for its ability to provide a stable vantage point to continuously monitor the Sun, making Aditya-L1 unique compared to other solar observatories that either orbit Earth or study the Sun from different perspectives.

📌 Additional Information

  • Lagrange Points:
    • Lagrange Points are positions in space where the gravitational forces of two large celestial bodies, such as the Earth and the Sun, balance the centripetal force felt by a smaller object.
    • There are five Lagrange points (L1, L2, L3, L4, L5). Aditya-L1 is placed at L1, offering a stable location for continuous solar observations.
    • Spacecraft positioned at L1 can maintain their position with minimal fuel consumption, making it ideal for long-term missions.
  • Solar Corona:
    • The solar corona is the outermost layer of the Sun's atmosphere, extending millions of kilometers into space.
    • It is characterized by extremely high temperatures, reaching up to 1-3 million Kelvin, much hotter than the surface of the Sun.
    • Studying the corona is crucial for understanding space weather phenomena that affect satellites, power grids, and communication systems on Earth.
  • Payloads on Aditya-L1:
    • The mission includes instruments such as Visible Emission Line Coronagraph (VELC), Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope (SUIT), and Plasma Analyser Package for Aditya (PAPA).
    • These payloads are designed to measure the Sun's magnetic fields, study solar winds, and capture high-resolution images of the solar atmosphere.
  • Impact of Solar Studies:
    • Understanding solar activities helps scientists predict geomagnetic storms and their effects on Earth's environment.
    • Solar research contributes to advancements in space exploration, satellite technology, and the protection of communication networks.
  • Global Solar Observatories:
    • Aditya-L1 joins the ranks of other solar observatories like NASA's Parker Solar Probe and the European Space Agency's Solar Orbiter.
    • While Parker Solar Probe approaches closer to the Sun, Aditya-L1's focus on the corona from L1 adds a unique dimension to global solar research.

📌 Important Points

  • India's First Solar Mission: Aditya-L1 marks India's entry into solar exploration, showcasing ISRO's capabilities in space science.
  • Launch Details: The mission was launched aboard the PSLV-C57 rocket from Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh.
  • Collaborations: The mission benefits from collaborations with scientific institutions like the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA) and the Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA).
  • Long-Term Goals: Data from Aditya-L1 will aid in developing predictive models for solar activities and their impact on space weather, benefiting both scientific research and practical applications.

  • The primary focus of the scheme is to encourage the installation of solar-powered pumps and grid-connected solar power plants in rural areas.
  • It aims to provide energy security to farmers by reducing their dependence on expensive diesel or unreliable electricity grids.
  • Under this scheme, farmers can install solar irrigation pumps and even sell surplus electricity generated from solar plants to the grid, thus generating additional income.
  • The scheme is divided into three components:
    • Component A: Setting up 10,000 MW of decentralized grid-connected renewable energy plants.
    • Component B: Installation of 20 lakh standalone solar pumps.
    • Component C: Solarization of 15 lakh existing grid-connected agricultural pumps.
  • The scheme also aligns with the government’s vision of achieving 175 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2022.

📌 Additional Information

  • Benefits of PM-KUSUM Scheme:
    • Reduces dependency on fossil fuels and promotes the use of clean energy.
    • Provides an alternative source of income for farmers by enabling them to sell surplus power to the grid.

    • Helps in achieving the government’s climate goals by reducing greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Key Features of Solar Pumps:
    • Solar pumps are powered by energy from the sun, which is a renewable and sustainable energy source.
    • Standalone solar pumps eliminate the need for grid electricity or diesel, reducing operational costs.
    • They are ideal for remote areas where electricity supply is unreliable or unavailable.
  • Implementation and Funding:
    • The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE).
    • It provides a combination of Central Financial Assistance (CFA), state government subsidies, and farmer contributions.
    • The central government provides up to 30% subsidy, while the state government contributes another 30%, and the farmer bears the remaining cost.
  • Global Perspective:
    • Globally, solar-powered irrigation systems are gaining popularity as a sustainable solution to water and energy challenges in agriculture.
    • Countries like India, Kenya, and Bangladesh have embraced solar irrigation to support smallholder farmers and reduce reliance on traditional energy sources.

  • By replacing diesel pumps with solar pumps, the scheme can save approximately 1.2 billion liters of diesel annually, reducing the import burden and foreign exchange expenditure.
