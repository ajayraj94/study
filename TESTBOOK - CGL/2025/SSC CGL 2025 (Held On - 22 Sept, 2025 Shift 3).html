Explanation:

The correct answer is Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle.

📌 Key Points

  • Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) is an expendable launch vehicle developed and operated by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).
  • It was first launched on 20 September 1993 and has since become one of the most reliable launch vehicles in the world.
  • PSLV is designed to place satellites into polar orbits, which are used extensively for applications like earth observation, remote sensing, and disaster management.
  • The vehicle is capable of delivering payloads to low Earth orbit (LEO), geostationary orbit (GEO), and sun-synchronous orbit (SSO), making it a versatile option for launching satellites.
  • PSLV earned the nickname "Workhorse of ISRO" because of its consistent performance, completing over 50 successful missions as of recent years.

📌 Additional Information

  • Key Features of PSLV:
    • PSLV uses a four-stage configuration with alternating solid and liquid propulsion systems, ensuring flexibility and high precision during launches.
    • The payload capacity of PSLV varies depending on the configuration, with a maximum capacity of around 1,750 kg to sun-synchronous orbit.
    • It can carry multiple satellites in a single launch, demonstrating its ability to cater to global satellite deployment needs.
    • Notable missions include the launch of 104 satellites in a single mission (PSLV-C37) in 2017, a world record for satellite launches in one go.
  • Applications of PSLV:
    • PSLV has been used to launch satellites for purposes such as earth observation, navigation, communication, scientific exploration, and interplanetary missions.
    • It played a key role in launching India's prominent missions like Chandrayaan-1 (2008), Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan) in 2013, and the RISAT series of satellites for radar imaging.
    • PSLV is also used for commercial satellite launches globally, making India a significant player in the international space launch market.
  • ISRO Overview:
    • The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is India's national space agency, headquartered in Bengaluru, Karnataka.
    • ISRO is known for its cost-effective and efficient space missions, cementing India's position in the global space industry.
    • PSLV is one of ISRO’s most successful projects, contributing to India's reputation as a reliable satellite launch provider.
  • Other Indian Launch Vehicles:
    • ISRO operates other launch vehicles such as the Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV) and GSLV Mk III, which are designed for heavier payloads and higher orbits.
    • Small Satellite Launch Vehicle (SSLV) is ISRO's newest development, aimed at launching small payloads at lower costs.

📌 Important Points

  • Global Recognition: PSLV's reliability and cost-effectiveness have attracted several countries to use its services for satellite launches.
  • Historical Achievements: PSLV contributed to India's first interplanetary mission, Mangalyaan, which successfully reached Mars in 2014.
  • Versatility: PSLV’s capability to launch satellites into different orbits makes it suitable for diverse missions, including scientific research and disaster monitoring.
  • Future Plans: ISRO continues to enhance PSLV’s capabilities, integrating advanced technologies to ensure greater precision and efficiency.

  • Beta-plus (β⁺) decay occurs when a proton in a nucleus transforms into a neutron, emitting a positron (β⁺) and a neutrino. This process is mediated by the weak nuclear force.
  • As a result of β⁺ decay, the proton number decreases by 1, while the mass number remains unchanged. This is because a proton is converted to a neutron, and both particles have nearly equal masses.
  • The emitted positron is the antimatter counterpart of an electron, carrying a positive charge.
  • The process leads to the formation of a new element with an atomic number lower by 1 than the original element, but the total number of nucleons (mass number) in the nucleus remains the same.
  • For example, during the β⁺ decay of carbon-11:
    • Carbon-11 (6 protons, 5 neutrons) transforms into Boron-11 (5 protons, 6 neutrons), with the emission of a positron and a neutrino.

📌 Additional Information

  • Other Types of Radioactive Decay:
    • Beta-minus (β⁻) decay: A neutron transforms into a proton, emitting an electron and an antineutrino. In this case, the proton number increases by 1, and the mass number remains unchanged.
    • Alpha (α) decay: The nucleus emits an alpha particle (2 protons and 2 neutrons), leading to a decrease in both the mass number (by 4) and the atomic number (by 2).
    • Gamma (γ) decay: The nucleus releases excess energy in the form of gamma radiation without changing its composition (proton and neutron numbers remain the same).

    • β⁺ decay is utilized in positron emission tomography (PET), a medical imaging technique used to study metabolic processes in the human body.
    • It plays a role in the synthesis of certain isotopes used in medical diagnostics.
  • Characteristics of Positron:
    • A positron is the antiparticle of an electron, having the same mass but carrying a positive charge.
    • When a positron encounters an electron, they annihilate each other, producing gamma rays.
  • Important Facts:
    • β⁺ decay typically occurs in proton-rich nuclei, where the proton-to-neutron ratio is too high for stability.
    • The weak nuclear force is responsible for β⁺ decay, as it governs the transformation of quarks within protons and neutrons.

📌 Important Points

  • Mass Number: The total number of protons and neutrons in a nucleus. It remains constant during β⁺ decay as no nucleons are lost.
  • Proton Number (Atomic Number): The number of protons in the nucleus, which decreases by 1 during β⁺ decay.
