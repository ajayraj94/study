The correct answer is The British monopoly on the manufacture and sale of salt.

📌 Key Points

  • The Salt Satyagraha, led by Mahatma Gandhi in 1930, was a nonviolent protest against the British monopoly on salt production and its taxation, which affected every Indian household.
  • The British government imposed a salt tax and prohibited Indians from collecting or making their own salt, forcing them to buy it from government sources.
  • On March 12, 1930, Gandhi and his followers began a 24-day march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi, covering 240 miles, to produce salt from seawater as an act of civil disobedience.
  • The march and subsequent acts of defiance against the salt laws galvanized nationwide support, symbolizing the broader struggle for Indian independence.
  • The Salt Satyagraha became a turning point in the Indian independence movement, showcasing the power of nonviolent resistance and gaining international attention.

📌 Additional Information

  • The imposition of heavy taxes on land revenue
    • The British introduced systems like the Zamindari, Ryotwari, and Mahalwari to collect land revenue.
    • These systems often resulted in exorbitant taxes, leading to widespread poverty and exploitation of farmers.
    • While this issue caused significant unrest, it was not the direct cause of the Salt Satyagraha.
  • The restrictions on the movement and assembly of Indians
    • The British government implemented several restrictive laws, such as the Rowlatt Act, which curtailed civil liberties like freedom of assembly and movement.
    • These restrictions led to protests like the Jallianwala Bagh massacre in 1919 but were not the primary focus of the Salt Satyagraha.
  • The introduction of separate electorates for different communities
    • The British introduced separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, and other minority communities through acts like the Government of India Act, 1919 and 1935.
    • This policy was aimed at dividing Indian society along communal lines but was unrelated to the Salt Satyagraha.
  • Overall Theme: The Salt Satyagraha
    • The Salt Satyagraha was part of Gandhi's broader strategy of nonviolent resistance (Satyagraha) against British colonial rule.
    • It demonstrated the ability of Indians to challenge unjust laws through peaceful means, inspiring similar movements worldwide.
    • The movement emphasized self-reliance, unity, and the rejection of oppressive policies imposed by colonial rulers.
