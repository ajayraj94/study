Explanation:

The correct answer is Stepped tank.

📌 Key Points

  • The Sun Temple at Modhera, located in Gujarat, India, is a remarkable example of ancient Indian temple architecture built during the reign of the Chalukya dynasty under King Bhima I in 1026-27 CE.
  • A defining feature of this temple is its massive and intricately designed stepped tank (Surya Kund), which served as a sacred water reservoir for devotees to perform ablutions before entering the temple.
  • The stepped tank is geometrically designed with terraces, symmetrical steps, and numerous small shrines carved into the steps, showcasing exceptional craftsmanship and symmetry.
  • The tank is divided into multiple rectangular and square tiers, with steps descending in a pyramid-like fashion, creating a visually stunning layout that enhances the temple's aesthetic appeal.
  • The stepped tank not only had religious significance but also served as a water conservation system, demonstrating the advanced engineering and ecological awareness of the time.

📌 Additional Information

  • Other Architectural Features of the Sun Temple:
    • The temple is designed in the Māru-Gurjara architectural style, characterized by intricate carvings, ornate pillars, and detailed sculptures.
    • The main sanctum (Garbhagriha) was constructed in such a way that the first rays of the sun would illuminate the deity, symbolizing the temple's dedication to the Sun God.
    • The Sabha Mandapa (assembly hall) is a pillared hall with exquisitely carved pillars and arches, showcasing stories from Hindu mythology.
    • The temple is divided into three main components: Garbhagriha (sanctum), Gudha Mandapa (main hall), and Kunda (stepped tank).
  • Significance of Stepped Tanks in Indian Architecture:
    • Stepped tanks, also known as Kunds or Pushkarnis, were a common feature of ancient Indian temples and served both religious and utilitarian purposes.
    • They were used for ritual purification, community gatherings, and water conservation.
    • These tanks often contained small shrines dedicated to deities, adding to their religious significance.
    • Famous examples of stepped tanks include the Rani ki Vav in Patan and the Chand Baori in Rajasthan.
  • Details about Incorrect Options:

    • Garuda Pillar: A tall pillar often dedicated to Garuda, the mount of Lord Vishnu, is typically found in Vaishnavite temples but is not a key feature of this temple.
    • Vimana: This refers to the tower above the sanctum in South Indian temple architecture. The Sun Temple at Modhera does not have a Vimana as it is built in the Māru-Gurjara style.

📌 Important Points

  • The Sun Temple at Modhera is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a popular tourist attraction.
  • The temple is aligned in such a way that during equinoxes, the first rays of the sun illuminate the idol in the sanctum.
  • The site is an excellent example of water management systems in ancient India, combining aesthetics and functionality.
  • The Modhera Dance Festival is held annually near the temple, celebrating the region's rich cultural heritage.

📌 Additional Information

  • Mathura School of Art:
    • It flourished during the Kushana period (1st–3rd century CE) and was centered in Mathura, Uttar Pradesh.
    • Known for its production of sculptures in red sandstone, it depicted Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu deities.
    • Key features include the depiction of Buddha with a distinctive smile, spiritual calmness, and ornamentation.
    • Examples include the Standing Buddha from Govindnagar and the Govind Nagar Yaksha.

    • A famous bronze statue from the Gupta period (5th–6th century CE), discovered in Sultanganj, Bihar.
    • It is about 2.3 meters tall and is one of the largest surviving bronze sculptures from ancient India.
    • The statue is currently housed in the Birmingham Museum and Art Gallery in the United Kingdom.
    • It represents the Gupta style of art, which is known for its refined and graceful depictions of religious figures.
  • Kushana Art Characteristics:

Given:

Tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal in length.

Calculations:

⇒ From point S, tangents SB and SQ are equal in length (SB = SQ).
