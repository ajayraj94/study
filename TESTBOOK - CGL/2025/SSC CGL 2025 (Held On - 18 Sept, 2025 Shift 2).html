Which of the following pairs of temple style & region are correctly matched?

1. Vesara - Deccan

2. Rekha-Deul - Odisha

📌 Key Points

  • Vesara - Deccan: Vesara style emerged in the Deccan region and represents a hybrid temple architecture combining elements of both Nagara (North Indian) and Dravida (South Indian) styles. It is prominently seen in Karnataka and adjoining areas.
  • Rekha-Deul - Odisha: Rekha-Deul is a specific type of Nagara style temple architecture found in Odisha. It is characterized by its curvilinear tower (Shikhara) and intricate carvings, as seen in temples like the Lingaraja Temple in Bhubaneswar.
  • Bhumija - Malwa: The Bhumija style is prevalent in the Malwa region (present-day Madhya Pradesh). It is distinguished by its vertically rising miniature towers arranged symmetrically around the main Shikhara, giving the temple a pyramidal appearance.
  • Kalinga - Tamil Nadu: The Kalinga style of architecture is associated with Odisha and not Tamil Nadu. Tamil Nadu is known for the Dravidian style of temple architecture characterized by massive gopurams and pillared halls.

📌 Additional Information

  • Vesara Style:
    • The Vesara style bridges the gap between the Nagara and Dravidian styles, often referred to as a mixed or transitional style.
    • Examples include the temples of Badami, Pattadakal, and Aihole built during the Chalukya dynasty.
  • Rekha-Deul Style:
    • Rekha-Deul is a component of the Nagara style specific to Odisha, with temples having tall, curvilinear towers called Shikharas.
    • Prominent examples include the Sun Temple at Konark and the Mukteshwar Temple in Bhubaneswar.
  • Bhumija Style:
    • The Bhumija style is notable for its intricate symmetry and miniature towers rising around the central Shikhara.
    • It was widely adopted in the western and central parts of India, especially under the Paramara dynasty of Malwa.
    • Examples include the Udayeshwar Temple in Udaipur (Madhya Pradesh).
  • Kalinga Style:
    • The Kalinga style is indigenous to Odisha and consists of three types: Rekha-Deul (curvilinear towers), Pidha-Deul (pyramidal roof), and Khakhara-Deul (rectangular structures).
    • It is distinct from Tamil Nadu's Dravidian style, which features large gopurams and open courtyards.
